Aviva adds terrorism cover as standard for digital SME policies in market first

Source: www.insurancetimes.co.uk · Score: 0.80
Why it matters: Aviva standardizes terrorism cover on SME policies; Pool Re incentive affects Lloyd's market exposure to terrorism risk.
  • Aviva has become the first UK insurer to include terrorism cover as standard on selected digitally traded SME commercial products (commercial combined, office & surgery, property owners, shop & salon, computers; contractors combined to follow) effective 24 Aug 2026, with existing policyholders receiving it at renewal.
  • Change follows Pool Re’s new incentive scheme that reduces reinsurance costs for participating insurers, enabling Aviva to bundle terrorism protection rather than offering it solely as an optional add‑on.
  • Market and distribution impact: only ~4% of SMEs currently buy terrorism cover, so automatic inclusion aims to close the SME protection gap, increase policy take‑up, and may shift competitive expectations for digitally traded SME products.
  • Operational and underwriting considerations: insurers should reassess pricing, exposure aggregation, reinsurance capacity and digital quoting flows if standardising terrorism cover; Aviva says it will review extending the approach to further digitally traded commercial products.

Source: www.reinsurancene.ws · Score: 0.75
Why it matters: UK captive regime and regulatory changes affecting Lloyd's market players.
  • PRA and FCA consultation (open until Oct 2026) proposes a bespoke UK captive framework for single‑parent captives, with legislation targeted as soon as July 2027 and planned future extension to group captives and PCCs.
  • Framework offers Solvency II exemption with proportionate, flexible capital and reduced reporting obligations—AM Best sees this as creating a competitive proposition for UK‑domiciled parents, though not necessarily materially more capital‑efficient than established domiciles.
