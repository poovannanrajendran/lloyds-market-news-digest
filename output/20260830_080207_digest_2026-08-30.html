ILS capital is "foundational" in reinsurance, grows to $144.5bn with 5-year CAGR 8.3%: Aon - Artemis.bm

Source: www.artemis.bm · Score: 0.75
Why it matters: Covers ILS and catastrophe bond trends shaping the global reinsurance market, relevant to Lloyd's.
  • ILS/alternative capital reached $144.5bn at 30 June 2026 (up $3.5bn in Q2), with Aon calling ILS “foundational reinsurance capital” after sustained growth.
  • Five‑year CAGR for alternative/ILS capital is 8.3%; the catastrophe bond market has expanded faster (11.6% CAGR over the last decade), cementing cat bonds as a core layer in many reinsurance towers.
  • Market innovation—notably asset‑intensive sidecars and whole‑account/casualty structures—is extending ILS capacity beyond catastrophe risk into broader P&C lines, supported by record issuance and some early maturities that freed capital for reinvestment.
  • Outlook: constructive — cedents will continue to seek diversified, multi‑year capital while investors demand disciplined underwriting, transparency and competitive returns; expect increased focus on asset‑driven sidecar transactions into 2027.

Underwriting discipline and risk transparency key as casualty ILS evolves: AM Best - Artemis.bm

Source: www.artemis.bm · Score: 0.72
Why it matters: Covers casualty ILS market evolution and risk/transparency concerns that impact Lloyd's reinsurance and market participants.
  • Casualty ILS is growing but remains a relatively small slice of alternative capital and substantially smaller than many earlier projections, with market evolution still closely watched.
  • Key structural challenges: casualty is long-tail with delayed loss emergence and exposure to evolving legal/social environments, making correlation, modeling transparency and investment duration harder to replicate than property catastrophe ILS.
  • Market and investor risks: some reinsurers have raised concerns about the pace and framing of certain casualty ILS initiatives — investors chasing yield may underestimate complex underlying liabilities, timing of payments and liquidity/early-redemption risks, which can materially affect returns.
  • Recommended focus: ensure underwriting discipline and risk transparency develop in lockstep with investor demand; although many casualty ILS deals are thoughtfully structured, isolated poorly designed transactions can impair perceptions of the broader asset class.

Why a blended reinsurance portfolio offers a compelling alternative to traditional ILS: Resolute Global Partners - Artemis.bm

Source: www.artemis.bm · Score: 0.72
Why it matters: Mentions Lloyd’s corporate member and market access in an ILS/reinsurance strategy.
  • Vertically integrated, multi-platform approach: the 1609 Fund accesses property treaty reinsurance (Bermuda, predominantly US risk), niche specialty (marine retro) and US primary insurance via Prospero Re, Producers National and a Lloyd’s corporate member—providing direct market access, structuring flexibility and tactical control.
  • Downside-protected portfolio construction: a proprietary 30+ year actuarial model plus portfolio-level risk overlays manage clash risk between distinct loss drivers, creating structural downside protection compared with traditional ILS pools.
  • Capital efficiency and mitigation of trapped capital: preference for doing more business on a rated Bermuda balance sheet rather than fully collateralized ILS; bespoke marine contract terms (up to three additional premium payments on reviews) force counterparties to release or commit capital rather than indefinitely trap it.
  • Underwriting discipline and selective exposure: focus on niche lines where they have deep expertise and relationships, strict exclusions (eg. war-on-land/Persian Gulf/terrorism), proactive reserving (eg. Baltimore Bridge) and growth within existing competencies rather than entering unrelated new sectors.
