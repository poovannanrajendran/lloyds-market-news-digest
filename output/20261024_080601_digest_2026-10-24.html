WFP secures $11.6m cyclone parametric insurance through CCRIF - Reinsurance News

Source: www.reinsurancene.ws · Score: 0.82
Why it matters: WFP joins CCRIF to buy parametric disaster insurance for Haiti and the Dominican Republic, signaling growth of parametric risk transfer in the Caribbean.
  • WFP has become CCRIF SPC’s first UN agency and multilateral policyholder, purchasing two tropical cyclone policies with a combined coverage limit of USD 11.6 million for operations in Haiti and the Dominican Republic.
  • The policies are intended to provide potential funding for humanitarian relief after qualifying tropical storms or hurricanes, subject to the contracts’ trigger conditions. WFP could use proceeds for food, cash assistance and other urgent support.
  • The arrangement gives WFP a separate route to potential disaster financing in the Dominican Republic, which is not a CCRIF member. In Haiti, it adds to existing sovereign insurance arrangements.
  • CCRIF says its membership now stands at 40. Its total coverage for the 2026/27 policy year is USD 1.8 billion, up from USD 1.44 billion the previous year; payouts depend on policy criteria being met.

Willis survey finds cyber security rising up boardroom agendas as data loss concerns persist - Reinsurance News

Source: www.reinsurancene.ws · Score: 0.82
Why it matters: Covers cyber risk governance and cyber insurance trends (adoption, budgeting, incident response) that are material to Lloyd's market insights.
  • Willis’s 2026 Directors’ & Officers’ Liability Insurance Survey found that 70% of organisations formally include cyber risk in board-level enterprise risk frameworks, and 65% brief boards on cyber security and the wider threat environment at least quarterly.
  • Overall, 60% of respondents said their organisation quantifies potential financial losses from a cyber incident: 19% do so annually and 41% on an ongoing basis. However, 55% reported a good understanding of their organisation’s cyber risks, while 46% considered their board sufficiently informed about the effectiveness of cyber security spending.
  • The survey found 48% of respondents held cyber insurance and a further 19% intended to buy cover within two years. Among policyholders, 71% had standalone cover; 48% included premiums in their cyber security budget.
  • Phishing and social engineering were the most frequently cited threats (48%), followed by ransomware (38%); third-party software vulnerabilities and misuse of artificial intelligence each received 31% of responses.

Mexico gets its first World Bank Cat DDO, to complement cat bonds and parametric insurance - Artemis.bm

Source: www.artemis.bm · Score: 0.78
Why it matters: Mexico's Cat DDO alongside existing catastrophe bonds and parametric insurance details; relevant to Lloyd's market coverage on ILS and disaster risk financing.
  • The World Bank Group has approved a US$500 million Catastrophe Deferred Drawdown Option (Cat DDO) for Mexico, the country’s first, to support responses to natural disasters, climate impacts and public health emergencies.
  • The three-year credit line, which may be extended, can be accessed almost immediately when an emergency occurs, without the Government needing to urgently reallocate its budget.
  • The Cat DDO adds a layer of financial protection alongside Mexico’s existing catastrophe bonds and parametric insurance, including its 2024 IBRD catastrophe bond arrangements.
  • The World Bank says the operation will support disaster risk management, climate resilience and policies aimed at preventing and reducing damage before disasters occur.

Source: www.insurancejournal.com · Score: 0.72
Why it matters: Discusses property claims trends and insurer market dynamics (labor, costs, AI adoption) affecting Lloyd's market context.
  • Sedgwick’s annual Loss Adjusting Insights report says property claims face delays and rising complexity, with labour shortages, equipment lead times and more complex losses putting pressure on timelines. Some specialist equipment lead times have more than doubled in five years; the report also expects a quarter of claim adjusters to retire by the end of 2027.
  • A survey cited in the report found 73% of respondents saw loss of industry knowledge as the greatest impact of the retirement wave, while 53% cited recruiting a new generation of talent. Upskilling junior workers and loss of company leadership were also concerns.
  • Verisk data showed US claim assignments fell to 1.24 million in Q2 2026, down 12.2% year on year and 13.1% below the five-year average. Catastrophe claims made up 43% of assignments, compared with 34% five years earlier; the report attributed the higher share to a sharper fall in non-catastrophe assignments.
  • ATI Restoration CEO Brant Wilson said higher deductibles and some commercial property owners’ in-house repairs had reduced jobs for restoration providers, while larger losses can take longer to manage when multiple parties review work and approve decisions. He said early identification and coordination of decision-makers can help keep projects moving.

Source: www.artemis.bm · Score: 0.72

  • The four-year, indemnity-based, per-occurrence reinsurance protection is due to run from 1 December 2026 to 30 November 2030 and covers specified perils including US tropical cyclones, earthquakes, severe thunderstorms, winter storms and wildfires.
