Lloyd’s issues framework for broker-originated reinsurance structures - Reinsurance News

Source: www.reinsurancene.ws · Score: 0.85
Why it matters: Lloyd's publishes guidance on broker-originated reinsurance structures with regulatory expectations and approval requirements; linked to Aon-Blackstone Cortina.
  • Lloyd’s has issued a framework setting approval requirements and ongoing oversight expectations for broker-originated reinsurance structures seeking to operate in the market. It follows news of Aon’s partnership with Blackstone on the vehicle codenamed “Cortina”.
  • The framework is anchored to Lloyd’s cross-cycle 95% net combined operating ratio target and covers risk concentration, ownership and capital, governance, managing agent capabilities, underwriting controls, portfolio oversight and eligibility.
  • Placements are initially capped at 10% of an individual broker’s treaty portfolio. Brokers cannot own or capitalise the structures; at least 19.99% of capital must come from the member aligned to the managing agent, and lead reinsurers must provide at least 25% of capacity.
  • Managing agents must demonstrate reinsurance pricing and portfolio expertise, have sufficient data access, and agree contractual guardrails with capital providers. Cedents must cede either all their eligible portfolio with the relevant broker to the structure or none.

Howden Re highlights reinsurance’s role as a capital tool as prices soften - Reinsurance News

Source: www.reinsurancene.ws · Score: 0.70
Why it matters: Discusses reinsurance as a capital-management tool and market conditions affecting insurers, relevant to Lloyd's market dynamics.
  • Howden Re says insurers should use reinsurance as a capital management tool, not only as protection, and reassess programmes in the softening, buyer-friendly market.
  • A benchmark of 42 European non-life insurers over 2023–25 found that the capital-light, high-return group held around €28 of capital per €100 of premium and averaged 22% return on equity; the capital-intensive, low-return group held €86 and returned less than 5%.
  • The report says strong underwriting remains essential: insurers with the best combined ratios averaged around 17% return on equity, compared with around 4% for those with the weakest. Capital efficiency amplified good underwriting results but did not create them.
  • Howden Re recommends reviewing retentions, transferring volatility where insurers lack a pricing or data advantage, protecting against accumulated exposures, and setting explicit capital and return targets. It also says investment risk should be considered alongside underwriting exposures.

Underwriting discipline and capital efficiency separating top insurers: Howden Re

Source: www.globalreinsurance.com · Score: 0.65
Why it matters: Covers reinsurance cycle, capital management and underwriting efficiency—key topics for Lloyd's market.
  • Howden Re analysed 42 European non-life insurers over 2023–2025, comparing returns on equity, combined ratios, solvency ratios and risk intensity.
  • The strongest performers averaged 22.3% return on equity, an 89.6% combined ratio and a 186% solvency ratio; weaker performers averaged 4.7%, 100.2% and 333%, respectively. Peer medians were 8.9%, 94.8% and 212%.
  • The report found underwriting performance was approximately twice as important as capital structure in explaining returns. Among high-return insurers with similar combined ratios, capital-light businesses averaged around 20% return on equity, against 13% for capital-heavy peers.
  • Capital allocation varied substantially: the capital-light, high-return group held about €28 of capital per €100 of premium, compared with about €86 for the capital-intensive, low-return group.
