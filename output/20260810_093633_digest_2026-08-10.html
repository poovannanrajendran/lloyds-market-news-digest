Drones set to deliver net risk benefits – but sky-high human error claims costs must be mitigated

Source: www.insurancetimes.co.uk · Score: 0.85
Why it matters: Drone risk management, regulatory context, and insurance market implications (MGA involvement, insurers) relevant to Lloyd's market.
  • Rapid commercial adoption: UK and global drone markets are forecast to grow strongly (UK CAGR ~8.2% to 2033; global ~9.5%), and PwC’s research finds drones can materially reduce net organisational risk by replacing hazardous tasks (eg working at height, confined spaces) and reducing exposure and site visits.
  • Human error drives most incidents: industry experts report pilot-related failures as the primary loss driver (estimates cited include ~95% of losses attributable to human error and ~50% of claims at one drone MGA linked to pilot error), with causes such as complacency, fatigue, distraction and overconfidence.

  • Regulatory and operational nuances: different permissions and controls (eg flights over crowds, indoor vs outdoor operations, drone swarms) materially change risk profiles; insurers need to factor evolving CAA/regulatory requirements, bespoke risk-transfer solutions and claims data into product design and pricing.

Insurers could drop vessel cover after Strait of Hormuz toll payments

Source: www.insurancetimes.co.uk · Score: 0.85
Why it matters: LMA guidance and model clause for marine hull underwriting on transit payments through the Strait of Hormuz, tied to sanctions compliance.
  • LMA has published a model clause and guidance for marine hull underwriters addressing transit fees, tolls and other payments (including non‑financial payments) connected with passage through the Strait of Hormuz.
  • Insurers incorporating the clause are not liable to indemnify such payments and cover will be voided where a payment breaches UK, US or EU sanctions or terrorism legislation.
  • The clause limits recoverable charges to legally permissible maritime/navigation services under UNCLOS and any applicable sanctions clause, effectively carving back cover for prohibited payments.
  • Purpose is to give underwriters and insureds a clear contractual position in a complex, evolving legal/regulatory environment and to evidence insurer due diligence and compliance.

Source: www.insurancetimes.co.uk · Score: 0.80

  • 575 commercial rooftop surveys (2024–2026) found 35% of buildings could not safely support PVs without work: 5% entirely structurally inadequate and ~30% workable only after remedial measures.
  • PV systems add multiple insurer exposures beyond fire (QBE: PV fire incidents rose ~60% 2022–2024) — key failure modes include panel uplift/movement, framing overload, water ingress at fixings and asbestos disturbance, all creating long‑term underwriting and claims risks.

Insurer actions: require declaration of rooftop PV, independent structural surveys prior to cover, insist on certified installers, adopt one‑in‑100‑year wind criteria at design, and mandate post‑storm inspections/documentation to reduce claims exposure.
