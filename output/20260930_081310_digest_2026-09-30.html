  • The Senate bill is a straightforward reauthorization; the House’s earlier version (passed June 30) differs by raising the insured-loss threshold for certifying a terrorist act from $5 million to $10 million (effective 2029) and requiring Treasury to provide notice during certification — those differences must be resolved in conference.
  • For insurers and brokers, the extension provides multi-year certainty for terrorism coverage and reinsurance placements, but the House-proposed threshold increase (if adopted) would likely reduce the frequency of federal certifications and could affect pricing, underwriting, aggregation modeling and demand for private reinsurance from 2029 onward.
