When a line is worth a billion: Wording lessons from the Russian aircraft judgment

Source: www.globalreinsurance.com · Score: 0.80
Why it matters: Discusses a landmark reinsurance judgment and its impact on wordings, covers, and market practices applicable to Lloyd's market.
  • Court held a lessors’ contingent trigger — responding where the insured is “not indemnified” — meant “had not been paid,” allowing recovery despite unresolved operator claims; single-line trigger wording can determine multibillion-dollar exposure and should be precisely drafted and negotiated.
  • Peril characterisation (war risks vs all risks) directly drove quantum because the covers had different limits; reinsurers must know how perils in their wordings map to limits and whether that allocation was intended to determine recovery.
  • The layered protection architecture (lease covenant, operator policy with AVN67B/AVN67C endorsements, cut-through to reinsurers, contingent backstop) was tested end-to-end; the cut-through is especially material for reinsurers since it creates direct claims by parties they did not underwrite and fixes limits of exposure.
  • Because the dispute turned on standard market forms, the public judgment produced market-wide clarity and prompt wording changes; the reinsurance market should weigh the trade-off between private arbitration and public litigation when a ruling would resolve systemic wording uncertainty.

UK Insurers Urge Bank of England to Rethink 'Dynamic' Stress Test

Source: www.insurancejournal.com · Score: 0.75
Why it matters: Mentions Lloyd's Market Association and impacts on the London market insurance sector; directly tied to Lloyd's market news.
  • PRA ran its first live, “dynamic” stress test (DyGIST) over three weeks in May, revealing scenario details in real time and simulating successive shocks (US earthquake, Gulf hurricane, UK windstorm, European floods, cyber event).
  • Industry feedback highlights significant operational strain: cumulative scenarios in a short timeframe were seen as unrealistic, generated heavy reporting demands, forced firms to bring in specialists and cancel leave, and pushed some to their limits.
  • The exercise was supervisory and mandatory for selected firms representing ~80% of the UK general insurance market (not a voluntary industry-wide drill); however, some practitioners (e.g., Lloyd’s CROs) found the live element valuable.
  • The PRA will publish findings by year-end and says it will consider industry feedback on resourcing and proportionality in its post-exercise review, so expect discussion on future format and frequency.
