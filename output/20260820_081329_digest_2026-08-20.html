Insurers Must Defend Hotels Accused of Sex Trafficking of Minors

Source: www.insurancejournal.com · Score: 0.72
Why it matters: Pennsylvania Supreme Court ruling on insurer duties to defend/indemnify and public policy; impacts coverage decisions and policy drafting, relevant to insurance market dynamics.
  • Pennsylvania Supreme Court held insurers may not refuse to defend or indemnify insured hotel owners/managers accused of enabling/profiting from sex trafficking on the basis of an "overriding public policy" where the policy is silent—criminalization alone does not create a judicial exception to coverage.
  • The court reaffirmed the broad duty to defend: an insurer must provide a defense unless the complaint and the policy language make it clear the claim cannot potentially be covered; this duty extends even to allegations that are groundless, false, or fraudulent.
  • The court declined to act as a "super-scrivener" to insert exclusions insurers did not write and limited reliance on the plurality opinion Minnesota Fire & Casualty Co. v. Greenfield, noting that unproven or unadjudicated criminal allegations against insureds do not justify denying coverage.
  • Practical takeaways for insurers: rely on explicit policy language (and draft specific exclusions if coverage for trafficking-related conduct is to be excluded), continue to consider reservations of rights and declaratory-judgment filings where appropriate, and anticipate potential coverage claims arising through insured bankruptcies or settlement trusts.

Responsive parametric solutions can help on aggregate SCS exposure: Descartes - Artemis.bm

Source: www.artemis.bm · Score: 0.72
Why it matters: Discusses escalating severe convective storm risk, insurer/cedant losses, and parametric reinsurance solutions affecting the (re)insurance market, including exposure and aggregation issues relevant to Lloyd's.
  • Severe convective storms (hail, tornadoes, straight-line wind) are increasing in frequency and intensity globally—especially in the U.S.—driven by a warmer lower atmosphere that raises low-level moisture and convective instability; industry losses from U.S. SCS have exceeded $35bn YTD 2026 and surpassed $50bn annually in recent years.
  • Exposure and vulnerability are rising (more development in rural SCS-prone areas, aging housing stock, repair-cost inflation), while cedants have kept aggregate retentions high, leaving primary insurers to absorb volatile losses and prompting higher deductibles, sub-limits and cosmetic-damage exclusions.
  • Traditional indemnity reinsurance towers often don’t fully cover aggregated SCS risk; Descartes argues parametric reinsurance can act as a complementary backstop by paying on a modeled-event-loss index for aggregated locations, helping protect net retained SCS losses over the year.
  • Descartes’ parametric approach models hail (NOAA radar), tornadoes (Enhanced Fujita data) and straight-line wind (physics-based atmospheric variables) independently to create rapid, objective triggers—making it a practical risk-transfer tool for insurers seeking to manage mounting SCS aggregate exposure.
