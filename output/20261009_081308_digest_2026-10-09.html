Fires and supply issues drive sharp increase in business interruption losses: Allianz Commercial - Reinsurance News

Source: www.reinsurancene.ws · Score: 0.80
Why it matters: Provides current business interruption loss trends and drivers (fire, cyber, supply chains) with implications for Lloyd's market underwriting.
  • Allianz Commercial analysed 7,888 business interruption claims worth about €6.74bn from 2021 to 2025. Average claims exceeded €850,000, around 70% higher than average property damage claims; claim frequency was relatively stable, while average claim value rose by over 30% annually in the past two years.
  • Fire and explosion accounted for more than 40% of analysed claim value, while natural catastrophes accounted for 34% and were the most frequent cause, at 26% of claims. Together, they represented over 75% of total claim value.

  • Ransomware remained the leading cyber-related cause of disruption, while cloud outages, software failures and incidents involving third-party technology providers also drove losses. More than 48,000 cloud and software outages occurred in 2025, according to the report.

  • The court rejected the employer’s argument that Warlow had to apply for rehire and prove he was rejected because of his injury. It said the burden shifted to the employer to establish reasonable cause after Warlow showed he was an employee, suffered a workplace injury and was denied rehire.

Economics of ILS and appetites of investors change with the reinsurance cycle: Moody's - Artemis.bm

Source: www.artemis.bm · Score: 0.78
Why it matters: Discusses Moody's insights on ILS/catastrophe bond pricing and reinsurance cyclicality—central to Lloyd's market dynamics.
  • Moody’s says excess reinsurance and ILS capital has pushed down traditional reinsurance pricing and catastrophe bond spreads, while the average expected loss of new catastrophe bond issuance has risen.
  • Spread compression has been sharpest for remote tail-risk layers; higher-frequency layers with greater expected losses have seen less compression. Moody’s says demand for these higher-risk protections is stronger while available capital is lower.
  • As low-risk catastrophe bond spreads fall, investors are committing more capital to higher-risk opportunities, including aggregate covers, frequency protections and secondary perils such as wildfire, flood and severe convective storm. Moody’s says remote-risk issuance has declined as some deals no longer meet investors’ return requirements.
  • Moody’s says ILS continues to offer good returns for investors and competitive pricing, multi-year pricing certainty, diversified capital and collateralised protection for sponsors. It identifies the extent of further pricing adjustment before the January 2027 renewals as a key question, noting that one major landfall could reprice risk quickly.

  • Otonomi Insurance Services, Tag-N-Trac and Solent Freight Services have launched TRACinSURED, a parametric cargo protection programme for perishable cargo.
