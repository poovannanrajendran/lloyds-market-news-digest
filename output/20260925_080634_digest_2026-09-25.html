Beazley launches new cyber endorsements to cover firms’ internal AI use

Source: www.insurancetimes.co.uk · Score: 0.85
Why it matters: Beazley, a Lloyd's market insurer, expands cyber/AI cover with endorsements.
  • Beazley has expanded its cyber and technology E&O offering to explicitly address risks arising from clients’ internal use of AI, not just external AI-enabled attacks.
  • Two new endorsements: (1) cover for voluntary shutdowns of malfunctioning AI systems to prevent significant financial harm, and (2) AI regulatory defence and penalties cover for certain AI-specific regulatory proceedings stemming from unintentional non‑compliance.

  • Market and capacity responses include ship‑to‑ship transfers at Port Said, Gulf producers buying/holding tonnage (older tanker prices > $100M), and Saudi appointing a domestic marine war-risk pool (Saudi Reinsurance) — these affect placement dynamics, capacity availability, and reinsurance treaty considerations.

Court bars shoulder injury damages in discrimination suit - Business Insurance

Source: www.businessinsurance.com · Score: 0.70
Why it matters: Court ruling on workers' comp and discrimination damages relevant to insurers and risk management.
  • NJ Appellate Division ruled a worker who received workers’ compensation for a shoulder injury cannot seek damages for that same injury in a subsequent discrimination/retaliation suit because the injury resulted from a workplace accident, not from discriminatory conduct or a failure to accommodate; the court reversed orders allowing bodily‑injury damages and admission of comp‑benefit evidence to a jury.
  • Case facts: Dane “Ali” Haskins injured his left shoulder in Dec. 2020, settled a workers’ compensation claim for $85,995 in permanent disability benefits (Nov. 2022), then sued alleging FMLA leave/ accommodation denials, wrongful termination and retaliation for filing the comp claim.
  • Court distinguished a 2021 NJ Supreme Court decision (where an employer’s failure to accommodate caused a medical condition) as inapplicable here; it also rejected arguments that a potential comp lien or alleged retaliation removed the exclusive‑remedy protection—Ekornes said neither it nor its insurer would assert a lien.
  • The case was remanded so Haskins may pursue other viable employment damages (lost wages, emotional distress). For insurers, the decision reinforces the workers’ compensation exclusive‑remedy bar for accident injuries and supports defending against double recovery and admission of comp‑benefit evidence in related discrimination suits.

Source: www.businessinsurance.com · Score: 0.65
Why it matters: Involves malpractice insurance coverage, policy terms, and duty to defend—topics relevant to underwriters in the Lloyd's market.
  • Federal court (W.D. Va.) ruled Hudson Specialty (Odyssey unit) has no duty to defend Dr. Mark Hormuz Dean in sexual‑assault litigation after finding he hid a prior sexual‑misconduct settlement from the insurer.
  • Dr. Dean answered “No” on 2011–2014 renewal forms to whether any claim had been brought for alleged sexual misconduct, but discovery revealed a 2010 demand letter and a $100,000 settlement; he later pleaded guilty in 2023 to multiple sexual‑assault counts.
  • The court emphasized the application question asked whether a claim had been brought (not whether he committed misconduct) and found the omissions material, voiding the policies for both the physician and his practices and eliminating the insurer’s duty to defend — even for related negligence claims.
  • Key takeaways for insurers and brokers: enforce precise application/renewal questions, perform targeted underwriting and renewal diligence (including checks for demand letters/settlements), educate insureds/agents on consequences of misrepresentation, and consider rescission/voiding remedies when material nondisclosures are discovered.
