Unpacking Warehouse Legal Liability

Source: www.insurancejournal.com · Score: 0.72
Why it matters: Detailed discussion of warehouse legal liability (WHLL), coverage triggers, contract effects, and CAT exposure—topic directly relevant to underwriting and risk in Lloyd's market.
  • Warehouse Legal Liability (WHLL) is intended to cover a warehouse operator’s legal responsibility for loss or damage to stored goods; physical loss or damage alone does not trigger cover unless the operator is legally liable.
  • A warehouse operator’s liability depends on the applicable law and the warehouse receipt, storage contract or service agreement. Contract terms can limit or expand liability and therefore affect what the WHLL policy responds to.
  • The article’s example contrasts a contract requiring negligence and limiting liability with one making the warehouse responsible for all losses. For the same £1,000,000 fire loss, the examples produce stated WHLL responses of £500,000 and £1,000,000 respectively.
  • A contract may impose liability for catastrophe events such as flooding, windstorm or earthquake even where the WHLL policy excludes those perils, creating an uninsured exposure. The article suggests obtaining first-party catastrophe cover or negotiating the broad liability wording out of the contract.
