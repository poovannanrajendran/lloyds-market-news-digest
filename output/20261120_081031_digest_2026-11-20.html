Effective practices: Cyber risk and resilience management relating to quantum computing

Source: www.bankofengland.co.uk · Score: 0.75
Why it matters: Regulatory guidance on quantum-related cyber risk and migration to post-quantum cryptography applicable to financial sector firms, including insurers in the Lloyd's market.
  • The PRA, FCA and Bank of England set out effective practices for managing quantum-related cyber risks. These complement existing cyber resilience guidance but are not formal regulatory expectations; firms should apply them proportionately to their size, complexity and risk profile.
  • A sufficiently capable cryptographically relevant quantum computer could undermine public-key cryptography used to protect financial transactions, communications, records and data, and to support authentication and digital signatures. No such computers are currently known to exist, but their arrival date is uncertain.
  • The regulators say firms have a strong case to prepare early: migration to post-quantum cryptography (PQC) will take time, attackers could collect encrypted data now for later decryption, and quantum technology may advance faster than anticipated.
  • Preparation should include identifying and prioritising cryptographic dependencies and vulnerabilities, assessing systems for upgrade or replacement, and managing risks involving third parties and connected organisations. Migration should preserve operational resilience and remain adaptable as threats and standards evolve.
