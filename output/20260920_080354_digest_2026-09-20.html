Reinsurance buying up 85% in a decade, but concentration at the top eases - Reinsurance News

Source: www.reinsurancene.ws · Score: 0.87
Why it matters: Mentions Lloyd’s top buyer status and its share of ceded premium; core Lloyd’s market relevance.
  • Europe’s 30 largest reinsurance buyers increased ceded premiums by 85% from €46.2bn (2016) to €85.6bn (2025) based on group-consolidated Solvency II/UK filings.
  • Top ranking was stable at the very top — Lloyd’s and Allianz consistently held first and second, with Lloyd’s, Allianz, AXA, HDI and Mapfre dominating the top five; below that the Top 30 was much more fluid (53 groups appeared across the decade, only 13 featured every year).

  • Individual cession rates vary widely (examples: Admiral from 60% in 2016 to 19% in 2025; AIG Europe from 25% in 2017 to 50% in 2025), yet the Top 30 aggregate non‑life cession rate rose only one percentage point over the decade (15% to 16%).

Guy Carpenter says Southern Europe wildfires highlight growing resilience challenge - Reinsurance News

Source: www.reinsurancene.ws · Score: 0.80
Why it matters: Wildfire risk and its implications for insurers/reinsurers, including coverage and treaty considerations relevant to Lloyd's market.
  • Insured losses to date remain relatively contained because the recent fires have mostly affected rural areas, but exposure and potential insured loss could escalate rapidly if blazes reach more densely populated residential or commercial zones.
  • Secondary impacts—business interruption, smoke exposure, evacuations, transport and supply‑chain disruption, and agricultural losses (crops/vineyards/livestock)—are increasingly the main economic drivers and create a more complex claims environment beyond direct property damage.
  • Coverage and reinsurance implications: in Spain private insurers cover wildfire property damage and Agroseguro covers insured agricultural losses; in France standard fire policies typically cover homes/commercial premises while business interruption depends on policy extensions; reinsurance response will follow underlying property/nat‑cat treaties and treaty wording for BI extensions.
  • Actionable takeaway: treat wildfire as a resilience, not just insurance, issue—enhance prevention, landscape management and emergency response and review underwriting, policy wordings and claims processes to address disruption as well as physical damage.

European Commission launches Climate Insurance Alliance - Reinsurance News

Source: www.reinsurancene.ws · Score: 0.76
Why it matters: EU Climate Insurance Alliance; 75% protection gap; implications for Lloyd's market
  • European Commission has launched the Climate Insurance Alliance to close a persistent protection gap (only ~25% of catastrophe losses in Europe are privately insured), aiming to shield national budgets from becoming the insurer of last resort and to act as the financial pillar of the EU’s resilience strategy.
  • Catalyst: a record-breaking summer of climate disasters (wildfires across Europe, ~660,000 ha burned, ~12 million tonnes of crops lost, critical river low flows and >35,000 heat-related deaths) and Europe warming at roughly twice the global rate, underscoring rapidly escalating insured and uninsured exposures.
  • The Commission will publish a climate resilience framework to identify 100 most vulnerable territories, mandate risk assessments, and set governance from EU to municipal levels—driving standardized vulnerability data, prioritized adaptation investments, and clearer regulatory expectations for risk disclosure and management.
  • Implications for insurers: heightened demand for tailored catastrophe and parametric products, expanded opportunities in underwriting adaptation technologies and resilience projects, increased need to reassess pricing, capacity and reinsurance arrangements, and stronger public–private risk-sharing/partnerships to protect sovereign balance sheets.

Casualty sidecars require discipline on both sides as asset risk grows: Ledger - Artemis.bm

Source: www.artemis.bm · Score: 0.75
Why it matters: Covers asset-driven casualty sidecars/ILS and capital/risk dynamics relevant to Lloyd's market participants.
  • Asset-driven sidecars are shifting focus from pure capital efficiency to integrated risk management: collateral is increasingly treated as a potential second source of return, so asset portfolios must be underwritten with the same discipline as the ceded liabilities.
  • While rooted in life/annuity markets, asset-driven structures are expanding into casualty—long-duration liabilities can match less-liquid private assets (e.g., private credit) and boost economics, but they also introduce additional asset and macro-sensitive risks.
  • Due diligence changes materially: investors and cedents must still verify insurance risk modeling, pricing and collateralization, and additionally assess how supporting assets will perform and provide liquidity across a full credit cycle.
  • Net impact for insurers: potential for extra capacity, capital flexibility and differentiated returns, but only if transactions have strong underwriting standards, governance, collateral mechanics and clear scenarios for stress, replacement capital and valuation interactions—evaluate these deals as both insurance structures and investment vehicles.

Discipline determines the credit outcome of soft reinsurance market pricing: KBRA - Artemis.bm

Source: www.artemis.bm · Score: 0.65
Why it matters: Discusses soft reinsurance pricing and management discipline implications for P&C insurers and reinsurance buyers, which impacts Lloyd's market dynamics.
  • Softer property catastrophe reinsurance pricing can boost earnings and strategic flexibility, but credit benefit depends on management choices rather than market conditions alone.
  • Optimal use of savings is to strengthen protection or retained capital (buy more cover, lower net retentions, reduce catastrophe exposure); using savings to fund aggressive growth, higher retentions, or shareholder distributions can be credit negative.
  • Rating effects will be company-specific: modest credit positives for stronger earnings; meaningful positives only if insurers materially reduce catastrophe exposure or build capital; negatives if abundant capacity leads to outsized growth without capital support.
  • Discipline and selectivity are crucial—maintain portfolio quality, prudent protection purchases and strong counterparty management—because management decisions will determine insurers’ credit standing when the market hardens.
