APAC cedents are looking for holistic solutions beyond pure volatility cover: Tony Gallagher, Marsh Re - Reinsurance News

Source: www.reinsurancene.ws · Score: 0.75
Why it matters: APAC re/insurance market dynamics, renewal trends, and growth opportunities relevant to Lloyd's market readers.
  • Marsh Re Asia Pacific CEO Tony Gallagher said clients are reviewing whether their reinsurance programmes are fit for purpose for 2027, focusing on retention levels, aggregate cover and protection for earnings and capital.
  • Gallagher said frequent floods, earthquakes and typhoons in Asia have largely been retained, prompting buyers to consider whether they need more effective portfolio and earnings protection.
  • He described the APAC market as varied, with conditions differing by country, territory and portfolio. Buyers are looking to purchase at least as much reinsurance as in 2026, and may seek more limit or lower-level cover.
  • Gallagher identified infrastructure and manufacturing investment in continental Asia, along with mergers and acquisitions among smaller insurers, as sources of increased insurance and reinsurance demand. He also said casualty and cyber insurance are growing, though more slowly than in mature markets.

Traditional BPO models could become ‘short-lived’ as 86% of insurance buyers rethink contracts

Source: www.insurancetimes.co.uk · Score: 0.70
Why it matters: Discusses AI-driven efficiency and transformation in the insurance industry, with implications for Lloyd's market players.
  • A study commissioned by mea Platform found that 86% of insurance buyers of outsourced operations would renegotiate for AI efficiency or rework contract terms and service levels if their contracts renewed today.
  • AI-led innovation fell below expectations for 49% of BPO users, the largest shortfall among the nine dimensions assessed. The study surveyed 150 insurance decision makers in August 2026 across 20 operational activities in North America, Europe and Asia.
  • mea Platform chief executive Martin Henley said insurers are pressing existing vendors to demonstrate how they can deliver transformation, and he expects traditional long-term BPO contract models to become shorter-lived and more focused on transparent links between price and value.
  • Henley said AI could help insurers process submissions faster and manage changing workloads, with opportunities beyond cost reduction including stronger relationships with clients or brokers and business growth.
