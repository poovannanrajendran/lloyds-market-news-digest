Insurance-linked securities attract DACH investor interest, allocations to rise: BAI survey - Artemis.bm

Source: www.artemis.bm · Score: 0.80
Why it matters: Covers rising investor interest in catastrophe bonds and ILS, a core insurance market asset class relevant to Lloyd's.
  • A survey of 105 institutional investors in Germany, Austria and Switzerland found that the share allocating to insurance-linked securities (ILS) rose from 9% in 2025 to 11% in 2026.
  • A further 3% indicated plans to enter ILS over the next 12 months, taking the reported allocation outlook to 14% if existing investors maintain their positions.
  • Among surveyed investors already allocated to cat bonds and ILS, the average allocation was 2.6%. On an assets-under-management-weighted basis, ILS represented 0.1% of the surveyed portfolios’ €2.9 trillion in total assets.
  • As a straight percentage of the total institutional portfolio, allocations to ILS and cat bonds rose from 0.2% in 2025 to 0.3% in the 2026 survey.

Fronting companies to engage more frequently with ILS market: AM Best - Artemis.bm

Source: www.artemis.bm · Score: 0.72
Why it matters: Discusses fronting, ILS, MGAs and capacity dynamics relevant to the Lloyd's market, even though Lloyd's is not named.
  • AM Best expects fronting companies to engage more frequently with the insurance-linked securities (ILS) market to diversify capacity, using catastrophe bonds, sidecars and collateralised reinsurance.
  • The US property and casualty fronting market grew during 2025, supported in part by expanding MGA-oriented business. Market estimates put fronted premiums above $30bn at year-end 2025; fronting specialists’ assumed premiums reached $4.8bn, up from $1.8bn in 2024.
  • Of the $4.8bn in assumed premiums written by fronting specialists in 2025, about $1.7bn was unaffiliated business initially underwritten by other companies.
  • AM Best says fronting can give ILS investors access to risks through rated insurers, but warns that these risks may be longer-tailed than traditional catastrophe risks and can involve investment assumptions, reserve-development uncertainty and operational conflicts. Fronting companies also need fall-back plans for after commutation.
