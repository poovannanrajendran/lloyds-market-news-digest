EU Commission proposes climate resilience framework, highlights cat bonds & reinsurance - Artemis.bm

Source: www.artemis.bm · Score: 0.84
Why it matters: EU climate resilience framework includes a Climate Insurance Alliance and use of reinsurance/catastrophe bonds—directly relevant to Lloyd's market focus on insurance and risk transfer in Europe.
  • The European Commission has proposed a climate resilience framework, combining a proposed European Climate Resilience Law with practical tools and measures for public authorities, businesses and other stakeholders.
  • The proposal includes a Climate Insurance Alliance to improve insurance protection and support risk prevention. It will bring together insurers, reinsurers, intermediaries, policyholders, risk-modelling experts, investors, EIOPA and public authorities.
  • The Alliance aims to deliver its first actionable measures by the end of 2027, including steps to increase insurance uptake, improve risk awareness and policy transparency, facilitate faster payouts, promote suitable and affordable cover, and encourage risk mitigation.
  • The Alliance will assess Europe’s natural catastrophe insurance capacity, including the potential for greater use of reinsurance and catastrophe bonds to cover natural catastrophe risks.

Cat bond risk spreads may decrease 10% in 2027, but market healthy and growing: Plenum - Artemis.bm

Source: www.artemis.bm · Score: 0.75
Why it matters: Catastrophe bonds and ILS market trends, spreads, and risk outlook—topics directly relevant to Lloyd's risk transfer and reinsurance.
  • Plenum Investments executives said catastrophe bond spreads have tightened by around 15% compared with last year. They expect spreads to decline by about 10% in 2027, subject to loss activity and capital-market effects.
  • Plenum expects the cat bond market to keep expanding and surpass $70 billion by the end of the quarter, with further new sponsors expected. The article also says issuance is on track to exceed $20 billion in 2026, which would be only the second year to do so.
  • Plenum described this year’s Atlantic hurricane season as the quietest on record by hurricane counts and Accumulated Cyclone Energy. It expects next year’s season to be more active, but does not expect the strong El Niño to increase or change cat bond market risk.
  • Plenum said spread movements vary across market segments: California wildfire spreads have fallen significantly but remain high, while spreads have risen in some other segments. The firm said this supports a selective underwriting approach.

SCOR sees competition from broker facilities, third-party capital as opportunity: CEO Léger - Artemis.bm

Source: www.artemis.bm · Score: 0.72
Why it matters: SCOR's discussion of market competition, third-party capital, and broker-led facilities informs Lloyd's market context.
  • SCOR reported third-quarter 2026 net income of €206 million, P&C insurance revenue up 3.5% to €1.9 billion and a combined ratio of 84.1%; it also cited deteriorating reinsurance margins amid a softer, more competitive market.
  • CEO Thierry Léger expects high competition at the 1 January 2027 renewals. He said SCOR will prioritise underwriting margin and quality over volume, deploying capital selectively where risk-return is attractive.
  • Léger said competitive pressure on margins has so far been partly offset by increased volume, with its effects expected to emerge gradually in SCOR’s quarterly results over the next one to two years.
  • SCOR views broker-led facilities and increased use of third-party capital as developments to assess and potentially turn to its advantage. The company already uses third-party capital through retrocession, risk-sharing arrangements and its ILS funds.

Brookmont's cat bond ETF surpasses $150m AUM. GTS now lead market maker - Artemis.bm

Source: www.artemis.bm · Score: 0.68
Why it matters: Catastrophe bonds/ILS ETF growth and market structure impact the broader insurance market, including Lloyd's, by affecting liquidity and access to risk transfer.
  • Brookmont Capital Management said its Brookmont Catastrophic Bond ETF (ticker ILS) exceeded $150 million in assets under management on 28 October 2026, up from $95.6 million reported in August.
  • The ETF moved its primary listing from NYSE Arca to the Texas Stock Exchange on 18 September 2026, and financial technology firm GTS became its lead market maker.
  • Brookmont said the fund’s ticker, name, investment objectives and investment strategies will remain unchanged.
  • Launched in early April, ILS was described as the first catastrophe bond fund strategy to be listed and traded on a US exchange.
