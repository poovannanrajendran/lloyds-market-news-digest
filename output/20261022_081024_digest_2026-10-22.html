Jamaica's parametric cat bond payout funded electricity restoration: Chief Fiscal Economist, Francis - Artemis.bm

Source: www.artemis.bm · Score: 0.83
Why it matters: Discusses Jamaica's use of parametric catastrophe bonds and ILS instruments (CCRIF, World Bank) and their market implications for disaster risk financing.
  • Hurricane Melissa in 2025 triggered the full US$150 million payout of Jamaica’s IBRD CAR Jamaica 2024 parametric catastrophe bond, with funds received within 30 days.
  • Jamaica used proceeds from the catastrophe bond and its cover with the Caribbean Catastrophe Risk Insurance Facility to help the Jamaica Public Service Company accelerate electricity network repairs in the west. Power had been expected to take until July 2026 to restore; 98% of the western side had power by February or March 2026.
  • Chief Fiscal Economist Terron Francis said the payouts supported response and recovery without emergency borrowing or substantial programme-budget reallocations, helping maintain fiscal and debt stability.
  • Jamaica’s debt-to-GDP ratio rose from around 62.4% to 65.5% after the event. Francis presented the experience as evidence of the benefits of planned disaster risk financing and rapid parametric payouts.

Cyber-insured SMEs 23 times more likely to have formal strategy as ‘education gap’ persists

Source: www.insurancetimes.co.uk · Score: 0.75
Why it matters: Covers cyber insurance market trends, awareness gaps, and broker opportunities relevant to Lloyd's market.
  • CFC’s study surveyed 501 UK SME decision-makers in June 2026 about cyber risk, preparedness and cyber insurance.
  • Nine in 10 cyber-insured SMEs reported having business continuity plans, and insured SMEs were 23 times more likely to have a formal cyber strategy. Confidence in managing cyber risk was reported by 94% of insured SMEs, compared with 61% of uninsured SMEs.

Why it matters: Global market rate trends and capacity dynamics that affect Lloyd's underwriting, though not Lloyd's-specific.

Deepening cat bond market offers investors liquidity, scale, and broader diversification: GAM’s Aviv - Artemis.bm

Source: www.artemis.bm · Score: 0.72
Why it matters: Covers catastrophe bonds/ILS market growth and sponsor activity, a core topic for Lloyd's risk transfer and market development.
  • Dr Orn Aviv of GAM Investments says catastrophe bonds have historically delivered returns with relatively low volatility and low correlations with major traditional asset-class indices. The Swiss Re Cat Bond Total Return Index returned 7.7% annualised with 3.7% volatility from February 2002 to March 2026, and recorded one negative calendar year: 2022.
  • Cat-bond losses are driven by defined insured catastrophe events, rather than corporate solvency, interest rates or economic cycles. Aviv says this gives the asset class different risk drivers from conventional equities and bonds, though cat bonds can also suffer drawdowns.
  • Cat bonds generally offer floating income linked to short-dated collateral and have maturities commonly around three to five years. Aviv says short maturities allow risk to be reassessed and repriced more frequently.
  • Issuance reached almost $18bn in the first half of 2026, while the outstanding market stood at $65.6bn. Aviv says the market’s growth supports greater scalability, improved liquidity and more scope to diversify catastrophe risk.

EU Officials Propose Guardrails for Insurers Participating in Unfunded SRTs

Source: www.insurancejournal.com · Score: 0.68
Why it matters: EU considers stricter rules on unfunded risk transfers for insurers, affecting securitization and Lloyd's market.
  • The European Commission is considering additional solvency safeguards for insurers providing unfunded significant risk transfers (SRTs), particularly if banks receive the most lenient capital treatment for these products.
  • An expert opinion cited risks including insurer default, disputes over whether a covered credit event occurred, and delayed compensation or payments. It referred to AIG’s difficulties during the 2008 financial crisis as a warning about unfunded credit protection.
  • The proposed safeguards include insurers maintaining solvency ratios above specified levels for the preceding three years, at least two protection providers per transaction, and provisions to replace a provider whose credit rating falls below a minimum threshold.
  • The note also recommends supervisory assessments of SRT concentration risks at banks and insurers, including potential effects on the EU financial system.
