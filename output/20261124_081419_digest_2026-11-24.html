  • The PRA’s 2026 Dynamic General Insurance Stress Test involved 25 major insurers, including the Society of Lloyd’s, representing more than 80% of the UK general insurance market. Its three-week scenario combined severe but plausible catastrophe and cyber events with financial market stress.
  • Reinsurance reduced gross losses from the scenario’s insurance events by around 72% and significantly mitigated the impact on insurers’ capital positions. Sector-wide reinsurance arrangements were found to be well-diversified.
  • The PRA highlighted the need for insurers to understand concentrations and dependencies in their reinsurance, including recoverability, counterparty resilience, reinstatement capacity and the availability of cover after a market-wide loss event.

Dynamic General Insurance Stress Test (DyGIST): 2026 results

Source: www.bankofengland.co.uk · Score: 0.75
Why it matters: Mentions the Society of Lloyd's and UK general insurance stress testing; relevant to Lloyd's market news.
  • The PRA’s Dynamic General Insurance Stress Test (DyGIST 2026) involved 25 major insurers, including the Society of Lloyd’s, representing more than 80% of the UK general insurance market. A three-week crisis simulation in May 2026 examined insurers’ decision-making and crisis-management capabilities; it was designed for learning, not as a pass-or-fail test.
  • Most insurers responded well. The PRA found that stronger responses mobilised relevant functions early, established clear ownership and escalation, made uncertainty visible in evolving estimates and showed how new information and challenge affected decisions. The exercise highlighted the value of testing how crisis and recovery plans work in practice.
  • For the events tested, each individual event left the aggregate sector solvency coverage ratio above 100%, and no participating insurer breached its Solvency Capital Requirement. Insurers’ qualitative assessments did not identify liquidity as a common sector-wide constraint. The PRA cautioned that these results do not provide a comprehensive assessment of resilience in all circumstances.
  • Reinsurance absorbed a substantial share of the losses. At sector level, no single reinsurer accounted for more than 10% of gross loss for any event, although the PRA said aggregate findings do not rule out concentrations or dependencies at individual insurers.
