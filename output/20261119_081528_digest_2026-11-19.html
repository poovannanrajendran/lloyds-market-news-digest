Private Capital Giants Target €80 Billion From EU's Insurance Regulatory Revamp

Source: www.insurancejournal.com · Score: 0.80
Why it matters: Solvency II reform and insurer investments in Europe—impact on the insurance market.
  • EU regulatory changes taking effect on 30 January will reduce capital charges for some securitised assets and other investments. S&P Global Ratings estimates Solvency II changes could release €80 billion of regulatory capital based on insurers’ existing holdings.
  • Some capital requirements for private equity and senior structured credit are set to fall sharply, in certain cases by more than half. Eligible long-term equities could receive a 22% capital charge, compared with 39% for ordinary listed shares and 49% for most private-equity and other unlisted investments.
  • Blackstone, Apollo and Pimco are seeking to offer European insurers investment opportunities as the reforms take effect. Insurers’ interest in securitised assets is growing, with collateralised loan obligations and private investment-grade credit identified as potential beneficiaries.
  • Insurers are not expected to shift portfolios quickly: they still need to approve mandates, build expertise and ensure less-liquid investments match liability cash flows. Due diligence, transparency, data collection and documentation can also make adoption challenging.

IFSCA working group finalises ILS and SPI regulatory recommendations for India - Artemis.bm

Source: www.artemis.bm · Score: 0.75
Why it matters: India's planned ILS framework (SPIs, catastrophe bonds) could affect global ILS/reinsurance markets, relevant to Lloyd's.
  • An IFSCA working group has finalised recommendations for bringing insurance-linked securities (ILS) under India’s insurance regulatory regime, including a statutory carve-out to enable fully collateralised special purpose insurers (SPIs).
  • The group recommends amending existing insurance law and developing dedicated regulations for SPIs and ILS in GIFT IFSC, covering catastrophe bonds, reinsurance sidecars and other alternative risk-transfer structures.
  • It proposes a phased approach: establish the legal framework and supporting market capabilities, with catastrophe bonds as the initial principal product; other structures could follow as the ecosystem matures.
  • The report calls for supporting expertise and infrastructure, including modelling, actuarial, insurance management, legal and transaction-management capabilities.
