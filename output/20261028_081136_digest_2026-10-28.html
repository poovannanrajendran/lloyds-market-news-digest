Bank of England warns climate risks could widen UK insurance gaps - Reinsurance News

Source: www.reinsurancene.ws · Score: 0.85
Why it matters: BoE speech discusses climate risk and insurance protection gap impacting UK insurers, pricing and availability.
  • The Bank of England warned that climate change could widen the UK insurance protection gap, leaving more households exposed to the costs of flooding as extreme weather becomes more damaging.
  • The Bank’s modelling suggests the share of UK mortgage borrowers without insurance could rise from around 5% currently to 7–10% by 2050, and potentially 16% after a severe flood event.

EU unveils climate resilience framework with re/insurance protection gap in focus - Reinsurance News

Source: www.reinsurancene.ws · Score: 0.84
Why it matters: EU Framework for Climate Resilience highlights insurance as a key element and proposes a Climate Insurance Alliance, with implications for the re/insurance market.
  • The European Commission has presented an EU Framework for Climate Resilience, aiming to embed resilience across EU policies, support local and regional authorities and encourage private-sector investment. It estimates €70 billion a year is needed to adapt Europe’s infrastructure and economy.
  • The Commission has proposed a Climate Resilience Law to establish common assumptions for future global warming, simplify national planning through one strategic plan per member state and improve the consistency of climate risk assessments. New online tools will help authorities, businesses and communities identify projected hazards and adaptation measures.
  • The Commission plans to establish a Climate Insurance Alliance to develop proposals for narrowing the natural catastrophe protection gap. Its first measures are expected by the end of 2027 and will address insurance uptake, prevention incentives, data sharing and available insurance capacity, including reinsurance and catastrophe bonds.
  • According to the Commission, around 25% of natural catastrophe losses in the EU have historically been insured. It says broader coverage could ease pressure on public finances and free up resources for preventive measures.

Modern warehouses test limits of fire protection - Business Insurance

Source: www.businessinsurance.com · Score: 0.72
Why it matters: Addresses warehouse fire risk and insurance underwriting considerations relevant to the Lloyd’s market.
  • Modern warehouses’ height, dense and automated storage, and increasingly combustible goods—including plastics, aerosols and lithium-ion batteries—are challenging existing fire protection systems and making fires harder to access and extinguish.
  • Experts say smoke and humidity contamination can add to losses when businesses discard affected goods; without a recognised protection solution, estimated probable maximum loss may be higher.
  • Insurers are asking more detailed questions about stored goods, fire detection and loss prevention. These factors can affect whether a business can obtain insurance, as well as its premium.
  • Changing storage configurations and combustible loads are prompting renewed use of in-rack sprinklers. Upgrading sprinkler systems can cost several hundred thousand dollars, and may exceed $1 million when larger fire pumps or water tanks are needed.

Biggest AI mistake insurers can make is automating bad processes – Shift Technology

Why it matters: Covers AI in UK insurance with UK markets context; not Lloyd's-specific but relevant to Lloyd's market readers.
