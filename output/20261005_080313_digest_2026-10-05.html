Prime London Homes Are Increasingly on Frontlines of Extreme Weather

Source: www.insurancejournal.com · Score: 0.80
Why it matters: Covers insurance risk/capacity (Flood Re changes) affecting high-end London property; relevant to Lloyd's market coverage.
  • Climate-driven heat and surface-water flood risk is increasingly acute for prime London homes—air conditioning is rare (<10% nationally), basic cooling retrofits are hard to install in high-end properties, and the Environment Agency estimates ~320,000 London homes/businesses at high surface-water flood risk.
  • Planned Flood Re reforms (from 2028) and existing eligibility limits (excludes homes built after 2009 and flats in buildings with >3 units) will reduce the insurer-of-last-resort backstop for many luxury and newer properties, tightening market capacity for high-value risks.
  • Market consequences: international buyers are deterred by perceived poor climate resilience and complex planning rules, contributing to weaker demand and price pressure for prime central London — creating transactions where insurance availability (or lack thereof) can block sales.
  • Implications for insurers and brokers: demand for granular, site-specific flood and heat risk assessment and bespoke underwriting is rising; carriers that invest in detailed hazard modelling and tailored products can win displaced business, while pricing and coverage terms will need recalibration for second homes and HNW exposures.

The Energy Market Playbook - Insurance Journal Research

Source: www.insurancejournal.com · Score: 0.72
Why it matters: Covers energy/renewables underwriting, data-center risks and policy shifts—topics Lloyd's market follows.
  • Federal policy shifts have altered the renewables landscape, but surging power demand—largely from data centers—keeps investment and new projects coming, forcing insurers to reassess energy exposure assumptions.
  • Underwriting playbooks are being rewritten: carriers are focusing on outage coverage for data centers, emerging battery-storage fire and degradation risks, and concentration/contingent business interruption exposures tied to critical infrastructure.
  • Market signals point to mixed near-term pricing: surplus-lines leaders remain optimistic while WTW/Ivans data show commercial rate moderation; however, underwriters should anticipate a potential energy-property pricing correction heading into 2027.
  • Broader claims and regulatory trends (AI-driven plaintiff strategies raising verdicts, TPLF disclosure advocacy, litigation examples) plus evolving workforce and life-sciences exposures mean insurers must tighten cross-line underwriting, reserves, and risk-management requirements.

AM Best downgrades SanlamAllianz Re ratings amid review of governance and capital position - Reinsurance News

Source: www.reinsurancene.ws · Score: 0.66
Why it matters: AM Best downgrades SAZ Re and places ratings under developing implications, signaling reinsurance capacity and governance risks that could affect Lloyd's market.
  • AM Best downgraded SanlamAllianz Re Ltd’s Financial Strength Rating to B++ (from A-) and its Long‑Term Issuer Credit Rating to bbb+ (from a-); both ratings remain under review with developing implications.
  • The downgrade follows an ERM reassessment to “marginal” driven by significant corporate governance and control weaknesses, including a USD 71m receivables write‑off equivalent to >100% of SAZ Re’s opening 2025 capital and surplus, plus discovery of legacy misstatements that delayed 2025 financials.
  • SAZ Re reported a USD 20.4m net loss for 2025 and further losses in H1 2026; shareholder injections of USD 47m (2025) and USD 27m (2026) proved insufficient. AM Best expects additional recapitalisation measures and is awaiting the company’s recap plan and 2026 year‑end statements.
  • AM Best still assesses SAZ Re’s balance sheet strength and operating performance as strong, but notes remediation of controls and governance is underway and untested — the effectiveness of these measures will be critical to the final rating outcome.

Leasehold reforms set to intensify scrutiny of insurance commissions

Source: www.insurancetimes.co.uk · Score: 0.66
Why it matters: Covers insurance commission reform and transparency in the insurance distribution chain, affecting the UK market including Lloyd's.
  • Leasehold reforms (following the Leasehold and Freehold Reform Act 2024 and the government’s July 2026 decision) are set to replace commission-sharing with a transparent permitted fee and wider transparency measures, increasing regulatory scrutiny across the insurance distribution chain.
  • Brokers and managing agents must evidence that any remuneration represents fair value and corresponds to legitimate, demonstrable services that directly benefit leaseholders — simply receiving higher commission is not acceptable without justification.
  • New disclosure and transparency requirements are expected (connections, remuneration arrangements, how quotes are obtained/selected, and standardised information for leaseholders), which may effectively end current commission-sharing and could influence premium levels.
  • Maintain rigorous documentation and review lease wording: firms should record remuneration decisions and service deliverables to show competitiveness and defensibility, and assess whether commission arrangements can be recovered from occupiers in light of cases such as the Trocadero judgment.
