Data centre outage risks create opportunities for parametric and non-damage BI cover: Howden - Reinsurance News

Source: www.reinsurancene.ws · Score: 0.78
Why it matters: Highlights insurance gaps and product opportunities (parametric cover, non-damage BI) for data-centre outages, relevant to Lloyd's.
  • Howden reports that half of data centre facilities experienced an impactful incident in the past three years. It estimates each outage costs $500,000–$1 million, with global annual losses of about $2 billion.
  • Power and backup failures, mainly involving uninterruptible power supply systems, account for most major outages, followed by cooling, network and IT failures. Howden says many incidents involve no insured physical damage, potentially leaving losses outside traditional property and business interruption policies.
  • Howden identifies potential solutions including parametric cover linked to service-level agreements and, where available, non-damage business interruption insurance.
  • US data centre power demand has risen by 19% a year since 2000, while grid capacity has expanded by 2% a year. Data centres’ share of US grid capacity increased from 0.1% in 2000 to 5% in 2025.

Insurance commission-sharing ban: Will leaseholders really benefit?

Source: www.insurancetimes.co.uk · Score: 0.78
Why it matters: Regulatory reform on insurance commission-sharing in UK leasehold market impacting insurers, brokers, and pricing.
  • Secondary legislation passed on 25 October 2026 will prohibit landlords, from 1 November 2027, from recovering insurance commissions and other excluded insurance costs through leasehold service charges, except for permitted insurance payments to be defined in further legislation.
  • The reforms are intended to improve transparency and give leaseholders stronger rights to challenge prohibited charges. Practical requirements for landlords and the definition of permitted payments remain unresolved.
  • Biba supports a level playing field but wants brokers to be able to receive fair remuneration from insurers for arranging buildings insurance, with that remuneration recoverable through service charges.
  • Industry observers question whether the changes will reduce leaseholders’ costs, warning that commission income could be replaced by direct fees for services such as insurance procurement, claims handling and risk management.
