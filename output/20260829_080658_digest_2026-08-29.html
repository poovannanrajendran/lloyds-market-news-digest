Reask study shows how pre-landfall parametric triggers fund pre-storm protective actions - Artemis.bm

Source: www.artemis.bm · Score: 0.74
Why it matters: Discusses advances in parametric insurance triggers and ILS-based risk transfer, directly pertinent to Lloyd's market underwriting and risk management.
  • LiveCyc is a probabilistic downstream forecasting system that converts agency forecasts into 1‑km neighborhood‑scale wind distributions (≈1,000 realizations), enabling site‑specific, probabilistic estimates for pre‑landfall decisioning.
  • Back‑tested on 16 major U.S. landfalls (2017–2024) against 1,840 surface gust observations, LiveCyc showed strong underwriting reliability: at Day‑2 (30–48 hrs) 87% of observations fell inside the modeled 90% CI, median bias 0.5 kt and CRPS 7.5 kt.
  • The probabilistic output allows parametric triggers calibrated to the cost of protective actions (illustrative thresholds: evacuating non‑essential personnel at an 8% chance of >60 kt; a ship sortie at 33%), so payouts fund anticipatory measures and materially reduce basis risk by tying the trigger to the same forecast driving the action.
  • Recommended product structure pairs a pre‑landfall forecast‑triggered payout (to fund protective action) with a post‑landfall realized trigger (to fund recovery), and Reask is already integrating these analytics with ILS managers—providing a practical route for insurers to offer automated, calibrated pre‑event covers.

California court says landlord protected from contractor injury suit - Business Insurance

Source: www.businessinsurance.com · Score: 0.66
Why it matters: Court extends Privette doctrine to landlords via chain of delegation, impacting landlord/tenant liability and insurance risk.
  • California Court of Appeal extended the Privette doctrine to non‑hiring property owners in the “chain of delegation,” reversing a trial court and granting summary judgment for a landlord after a tenant’s independent contractor fell through a skylight.
  • Court reasoning: the lease expressly allowed the tenant to install/operate/maintain equipment, the tenant hired the contractor and the contractor knew of the skylights, so imposing greater liability on the landlord (which did not control the work) would be inequitable.
  • Implication for insurers: landlords can be shielded from contractors’ workplace injury claims when leases contemplate contractor work and tenants hire independent contractors, reducing landlord liability exposure absent recognized exceptions to Privette.
  • Recommended insurer actions: scrutinize lease language, indemnity and insurance obligations, tenant/vendor vetting and contractor classification during underwriting and risk control; monitor for factual or legal exceptions to Privette that could reopen landlord liability.
