Separate AI systems could create hidden links between losses: Zurich - Reinsurance News

Source: www.reinsurancene.ws · Score: 0.79
Why it matters: Discusses systemic AI risk and insurability in the insurance industry, with implications for insurers and markets including Lloyd's.
  • A Zurich and University of St. Gallen report warns that using different AI providers does not necessarily make risks independent: systems may share underlying weaknesses and fail in similar ways.
  • The report distinguishes concentration risk, where organisations depend on the same providers, models or infrastructure, from correlation risk, where separate systems share data, architecture, assumptions or decision logic. Correlated losses may emerge gradually through claims, regulatory action or litigation across unrelated policyholders.
  • The research is based on 50 expert interviews conducted between July and September 2026 and maps AI exposure across four risk zones, from contained risks to systemic risks with widespread, hard-to-trace impacts.
  • The report recommends mapping dependencies beyond direct suppliers, setting system limits, assigning accountability, and testing the ability to override AI, maintain critical operations or switch to alternatives. It says insurability improves when exposures and losses are measurable and bounded, accountability is clear, and risks can be diversified.
