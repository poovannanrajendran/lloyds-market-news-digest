CP14/26 – Review of the Senior Managers and Certification Regime (SM&CR) – Phase 2

Source: www.bankofengland.co.uk · Score: 0.80
Why it matters: Covers PRA SM&CR Phase 2 reforms affecting senior managers and the certification regime, relevant to Lloyd's market participants.
  • The PRA and FCA are consulting on Phase 2 reforms to the Senior Managers and Certification Regime, aiming to preserve individual accountability while reducing regulatory burden and making appointments more flexible.
  • The PRA proposes to replace pre-approval with notification for almost half of Senior Manager Functions, including Chief Actuaries and Chairs of Risk and Audit Committees; firms would remain responsible for candidates’ fitness and propriety.
  • The proposals would remove PRA Certification Regime fitness-and-propriety assessments on appointment and annually, certificate issuance and regulatory references for around 7,700 individuals in scope.
  • Other proposals include extending temporary SMF cover from 12 to 18 weeks, reducing reporting frequency and removing some Prescribed Responsibilities. Significant reforms depend on Parliament enacting the Financial Services and Markets Bill 2026 substantially in its current form.

Brazil's reinsurance market to benefit from new tax changes: Fitch - Reinsurance News

Why it matters: Brazil tax reform impacting local/international reinsurers, captives, and retrocession; relevant to Lloyd's market.
  • Brazil’s Law No. 15,525/2026 reduces local reinsurers’ CSLL rate from 15% to 9% in 2027 and removes the 10% corporate income tax surcharge in 2030. Fitch says the maximum nominal tax rate on local reinsurers’ earnings will fall from 40% to 34% in 2027–2029 and 24% from 2030; primary commercial insurers remain subject to a 40% ceiling.
  • The law may make onshore captive reinsurers more attractive and encourage groups to retain more risk in Brazil. Fitch does not expect the tax change alone to cause a broad shift of ceded risks from foreign reinsurers, and says it is not by itself a rating driver.
  • Fitch says any credit benefit will depend on execution, capital support, retrocession and whether structures strengthen consolidated financial profiles. Tax savings must outweigh operating, capital, governance, compliance and retrocession costs; insufficient diversification or retrocession could put pressure on credit profiles.
  • In the first half of 2026, ceded premiums totalled BRL15.27bn, or 13.4% of insurance-market premiums excluding pension and savings bond business. Local reinsurers received BRL8.08bn (52.9%) and foreign reinsurers BRL7.19bn (47.1%).
