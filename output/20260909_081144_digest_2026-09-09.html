Delegated underwriting reshapes global insurance markets, Moody's finds - Reinsurance News

Source: www.reinsurancene.ws · Score: 0.85
Why it matters: Moody’s analysis of delegated underwriting and its impact on Lloyd’s premium income, business model, and profitability.
  • Delegated underwriting (MGAs, Lloyd’s cover holders, broker facilities) has expanded rapidly—MGA-written premiums more than doubled from 2020–2024 and delegated authority now represents ~40% of Lloyd’s premium—driven by demand for specialist/complex risks (cyber, climate, political) and insurers’ desire to access niche segments without heavy in-house investment.
  • Benefits include faster growth, diversification and efficient access to specialist underwriting and predictable premium flows via broker facilities, allowing carriers to scale into underserved or complex markets with lower upfront capability costs.
  • Key risks: greater reliance on third-party underwriters can erode insurers’ profit margins, weaken underwriting control and governance, shift economic value and customer relationships toward distributors, and increase commoditisation of carriers—risks amplified by rising private equity activity in the MGA sector and softening commercial markets.
  • Moody’s recommendation for insurers: strengthen oversight, governance and risk-management frameworks for delegated partners, preserve underwriting discipline and pricing governance, and monitor concentration, reputational and capital-allocation risks as delegated models expand.
