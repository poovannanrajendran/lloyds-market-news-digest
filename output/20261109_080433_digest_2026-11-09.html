2001 CAT Re Ltd. (Series 2026-1)

Source: www.artemis.bm · Score: 0.80
Why it matters: Mentions Lloyd's syndicate 2232 and Allied World's cat bond; relevant to Lloyd's market activity.
  • Allied World is sponsoring its third catastrophe bond through Bermuda-based 2001 CAT Re Ltd., with three tranches intended to renew and expand on protection from its 2023 bond.
  • The Series 2026-1 notes target $325 million of limit and are intended to protect Allied World and certain underwriting subsidiaries against US named storms, US and Canada earthquakes, and European windstorms.
  • The bond provides annual aggregate protection across three risk periods starting 1 January 2027, using weighted industry-loss index triggers measured by PCS and PERILS.
  • The tranches are $225 million Class A, $50 million Class B and $50 million Class C, with attachment indices of $450 million, $375 million and $300 million respectively; each has a $50 million franchise deductible.

Managing Safety Begins Before the Crash: Why Better Trucking Outcomes Depend on Being Proactive

Source: www.insurancejournal.com · Score: 0.72
Why it matters: Covers U.S. trucking insurance market dynamics, pricing, excess liability costs, tort reform impact, and a Supreme Court case affecting freight brokers—direct relevance to Lloyd's market underwriting and risk pricing.
  • ATRI research found that US trucking liability premiums rose 18.6% per mile from 2021 to 2024, while per-mile liability losses among respondents increased by 33.1%; heavy-duty truck-involved crash rates fell 2.6%.
  • Excess liability premiums rose faster: per-mile costs increased 34% for the $5–$10 million layer and 45% for the $10–$15 million layer between 2021 and 2024. ATRI cited litigation as a factor in rising claims costs.
  • A broker said tort reform in Florida and Georgia had helped moderate rate increases, but inflation in vehicle repair costs and other loss-cost pressures meant significant reductions in commercial auto rates were not expected soon.
  • ATRI reported that the average cost of operating a truck reached $2.336 per mile in 2025, up 3.4%. A broker said fluctuating fuel costs and freight shifting from long-haul trucking to rail were adding pressure, particularly for smaller fleets and owner-operators.

Risk professionals want to know: Will insurance survive, thrive or die?

Source: www.insurancetimes.co.uk · Score: 0.68
Why it matters: Industry-wide risk management focus and insights from major insurers; aligns with market news themes.
  • At Ferma Forum 2026, HDI Global’s Mukadder Erdönmez argued that insurers should shift from relying mainly on risk transfer towards predicting and preventing claims, while recognising that models cannot account for every event.
  • Erdönmez said climate change, AI and geopolitics are interconnected risks that can make clients’ risks and losses more complex and severe. She cited property modelling, analysis of the Representative Actions Directive’s potential impact on clients, and cyber risk engineering as examples of predictive and preventive work.
  • Swiss Re Corporate Solutions’ Ole Ohlmeyer said insurers risk becoming obsolete if they focus on products rather than helping clients make risks manageable. He described insurance as one layer in a wider resilience ecosystem, supported by technology, data, risk expertise and human judgement.
  • Ohlmeyer said insurers can help clients understand risks and weaknesses and intervene where it matters, while noting that some events, such as hurricanes or supply-chain failures, cannot be stopped or reliably predicted.

Source: www.insurancejournal.com · Score: 0.65
Why it matters: Covers rising weather-related insured losses and insurance market responses relevant to Lloyd's market.
