25 Years Later: IJ's Past and Continued Coverage of the Insurance Impact of 9/11

Source: www.insurancejournal.com · Score: 0.82
Why it matters: Covers terrorism risk insurance, TRIA, and post-9/11 market impacts—topics central to Lloyd's market news.
  • September 11, 2001 led to nearly 3,000 fatalities and about $60 billion in insured losses (in today’s dollars), prompting insurers to carve out terrorism exposure and revealing the private market couldn’t absorb extreme terrorism losses alone.
  • The Terrorism Risk Insurance Act (TRIA), enacted in late 2002, created a federal backstop: insurers must offer terrorism coverage and the federal government steps in for a Treasury‑certified event that exceeds specified thresholds (an event threshold cited at $5 million and $200 million in industry losses) after each insurer meets a 20% deductible; TRIA has never been triggered but enabled large developments to be financed and insured.
  • TRIA has been reauthorized multiple times (most recently in 2019); the House overwhelmingly passed HR 7128 (373‑15) to extend the public‑private partnership through 2034 and to raise the threshold to $10 million in 2029 — the industry is pressing the Senate and president to complete reauthorization before the current program expires on Dec. 31, 2027.
  • Key takeaways for insurers: terrorism risk has evolved (including cyber and systemic scenarios), so firms must prioritize preparedness, resilience and adaptive risk‑management tools while supporting continuation of the TRIA public‑private backstop.
