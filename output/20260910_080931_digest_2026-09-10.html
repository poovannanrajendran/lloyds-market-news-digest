Briefing: How to lose friends and alienate Rolex wearers

Source: www.insurancetimes.co.uk · Score: 0.84
Why it matters: Discusses underwriting, moral hazard, and market practices in the Lloyd's market (mentions Lloyd's/Leadenhall Market).
  • Visible signals of wealth (e.g., conspicuous watches, cars, social media posts) create measurable moral‑hazard exposure that can make otherwise similar risks materially different for underwriters.
  • Conventional responses (higher/variable excesses, warranties, stricter security, claims feedback into pricing) remain valid but offer limited competitive differentiation.
  • A potential advantage lies in dynamically quantifying behaviour‑based factors (public display, storage discipline, travel patterns, security adherence, prior losses) and using explainable incentives—eg, premium credits or short security briefings—to shape safer insured behaviour.
