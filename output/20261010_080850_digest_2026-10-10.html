Source: www.businessinsurance.com · Score: 0.78
Why it matters: Aviation safety failure report with insurer-risk implications for aviation insurance and risk management.
  • A US Army report found systemic failures contributed to the January 2025 collision near Ronald Reagan Washington National Airport, which killed 67 people. It identified two failed radio transmissions as the key factor, leaving the helicopter crew without critical air traffic control information.
  • The report also cited technical problems with the Black Hawk, insufficient semiannual flying hours among helicopter crews, and many battalion helicopters lacking collision-avoidance systems. The helicopter’s ADS-B Out location data was unavailable because of improper maintenance.
  • The Army report criticised shortcomings in Army–FAA work on Washington-area helicopter incidents and FAA air traffic control’s use of non-standard terminology and incomplete traffic advisories.   • The Army said it had strengthened training and surveillance checks, completed helicopter safety inspections and altitude-awareness testing, and was pursuing an FAA data-sharing agreement. The NTSB said the Army had made significant progress on its recommendations but still needed further work.
