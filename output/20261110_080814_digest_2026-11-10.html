One war, two books: Aggregation crossing from aviation into marine

Source: www.globalreinsurance.com · Score: 0.84
Why it matters: War-risk losses, aggregation, and wordings implications directly impact Lloyd's market and reinsurance.
  • Reinsurance aggregation disputes may follow war-risk losses involving aircraft stranded in Russia, vessels detained in Ukrainian ports and Red Sea shipping casualties; the article says these losses share a geopolitical origin but may be treated differently under treaty wordings.
  • The key first question is which reinsurance book covers a loss: marine or war and political-violence cover. That classification determines which treaty and aggregation wording applies, and may depend on direct-market coverage findings.
  • Existing aggregation tests were developed for events such as natural catastrophes and terrorism. The article says they were not designed for state-directed appropriation across a jurisdiction or a months-long campaign of separate attacks along a shipping corridor.
  • The article identifies unresolved aggregation questions: whether the Russian aircraft losses amount to one event or many; how the individually ripening Black Sea vessel losses should be counted; and whether Red Sea attacks constitute one or multiple events under different treaty wordings.
