PHP vulnerability raises fears of systemic cyber claims

Source: www.insurancetimes.co.uk · Score: 0.82
Why it matters: Covers cyber risk from PHP vulnerabilities and insurance implications for the London market/Lloyd's
  • The NCSC urged organisations to identify systems running PHP and apply updates for two disclosed vulnerabilities, CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619, which could let unauthenticated attackers execute commands on web servers or access sensitive information. It advised considering taking affected systems offline where they cannot be patched or are only temporarily mitigated.
  • The SIR said PHP underpins roughly 70% of websites with identifiable underlying technology. CyberCube reported millions of attack attempts but had not confirmed successful breaches; it also warned that AI had helped turn a long-standing flaw into a working exploit.
  • Cowbell’s Simon Hughes said businesses may not know whether their websites or customer portals use PHP. He urged them to check with hosting providers and for businesses and brokers to review policy terms on voluntary shutdowns and waiting periods.
  • HF’s Graham Ludlam said vulnerability identification and patching are generally day-to-day IT costs and would not typically be covered by London market cyber policies, which usually respond after a relevant event. He warned of potential accumulated claims if exploitation affected multiple organisations, and possible management liability exposure if known vulnerabilities were left unaddressed in breach of directors’ duties.
