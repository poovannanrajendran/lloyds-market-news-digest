Lloyd's, LMA & IUA highlight resilience of UK insurance market following PRA stress test - Reinsurance News

Source: www.reinsurancene.ws · Score: 0.92
Why it matters: Covers PRA's 2026 DyGIST results and responses from Lloyd's, LMA and IUA, highlighting market resilience and implications for Lloyd's market.
  • The PRA’s three-week DyGIST exercise in May 2026 involved 25 major insurers, representing more than 80% of the UK general insurance market, and tested responses to severe, evolving insurance and financial-market stresses.
  • Lloyd’s, the LMA and the IUA welcomed the findings, highlighting market resilience. The IUA said sector capital stayed above the regulatory solvency threshold and no participating insurer breached its capital requirement.
  • Lloyd’s said it remained able to meet valid claims and continue operating, and tested assumptions about recovery and recapitalisation. It identified the availability, timing and effectiveness of recapitalisation as dependent on market conditions, capital-provider appetite and the underwriting cycle.
  • The LMA highlighted lessons around rapid approval of capital reload and post-event growth plans, access to data, and monitoring the continued availability of group support. It also said firms benefited from practising crisis-management and escalation triggers.

PRA’s DyGIST exercise ‘went well beyond conventional stress test’

Source: www.insurancetimes.co.uk · Score: 0.82
Why it matters: Mentions Lloyd's participation and its disaster-response framework within the DyGIST 2026 stress test and related market resilience discussion.
  • The PRA’s DyGIST 2026 simulated a sequence of severe but plausible events over three weeks in May, requiring insurers to assess impacts on their financial positions and activate crisis-management responses in near real time.
  • Published on 23 November 2026, the results found that the market downturn was the largest driver of capital erosion and affected all participating insurers. Despite reduced capital coverage, the sector remained above regulatory capital requirements in aggregate, and no participant reported material derivatives-related margin or collateral pressure from the downturn.
  • The exercise found that firms’ crisis and risk-management frameworks generally stood up well, identifying areas for refinement rather than wholesale redesign. Lloyd’s said its disaster response framework operated effectively and that the exercise helped test assumptions about market recovery and recapitalisation.
  • The PRA will use the findings to consider further supervisory focus on catastrophe loss estimation, reinsurance dependencies and the practical executability of group support under stress. DyGIST 2026 will also inform ongoing work on preparedness, recovery planning and operational resilience.

Source: www.artemis.bm · Score: 0.70
Why it matters: Covers catastrophe bonds and regulatory debate affecting ILS/insurance markets (relevant to Lloyd's market).
  • Plenum Investments’ Daniel Grieger criticises ESMA’s proposal to cap catastrophe bond allocations in UCITS funds at 10%, arguing that its risk assessment relies on isolated events and does not adequately compare catastrophe bonds with other asset classes.
  • Grieger cites historical figures showing a maximum loss of 15.3% for the Swiss Re Cat Bond Index TR, compared with 57.1% for the MSCI World Net TR Index; recovery took 8.5 months for catastrophe bonds and 5.6 years for equities.
  • He says catastrophe bond funds have generally held more than 100 positions, maintained liquidity buffers of 5–10% and experienced relatively stable returns. He also notes that ESMA had granular portfolio data for only five funds when proposing the cap.
  • Grieger says catastrophe bonds’ short maturities allow risk models, attachment points and spreads to be repriced regularly. Since 2003, realised losses averaged 0.93% a year, below the modelled expected loss of 2.3% a year.
