Lloyd’s is seeing fresh routes to capacity — and a closer link between underwriting and reinsurance capital.

Today’s digest points to new syndicate plans alongside broader questions about broker-originated structures and specialist capacity. For London Market firms, these developments could shape how risk and capital are brought together, subject to approvals and final details.

1) Lloyd’s guidance on broker-originated reinsurance structures - Why it matters: It sets expectations for arrangements that may become more common as demand grows.
2) Generali’s planned Syndicate 2031 Red Bridge - Why it matters: The syndicate is intended to assume a share of Generali’s outward reinsurance from 2027, subject to requirements.
3) Credeq Syndicate 1998 - Why it matters: Its proposed focus on credit risk, surety reinsurance and European trade credit could add specialist capacity from 1 January 2027, subject to final permission.
4) Subscribe’s £5m binder with Fortegra - Why it matters: The agreement provides A-rated capacity for UK micro and SME professional indemnity and is intended to support further specialist products.
