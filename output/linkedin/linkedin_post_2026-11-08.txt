A new Lloyd’s vehicle backed by Blackstone and Aon is today’s clearest London Market signal: Alternative capital is finding another route into reinsurance.

The wider picture is mixed: Catastrophe bonds are becoming cheaper, while rising secondary-peril losses may prompt insurers to reassess how their reinsurance is structured.

1) Cortina, Lloyd’s Syndicate 1956 - Why it matters: It will provide capital backing reinsurance contracts, taking shares of eligible Aon-arranged deals, with QBE responsible for underwriting and governance.
2) Allianz Commercial’s planned Lloyd’s syndicate - Why it matters: A new underwriting syndicate could add to Lloyd’s capacity and competition, though its timing, lines and planned capacity are not specified.
3) MS Amlin syndicate’s first cat bond - Why it matters: The proposed $100 million bond would add another catastrophe-risk transfer route for a Lloyd’s syndicate.
4) Secondary-peril losses and reinsurance structures - Why it matters: Marsh Re says severe convective storms, wildfire, winter storms and flood are significant contributors to North American insured catastrophe losses, potentially prompting a rethink of programmes focused on rarer, larger events.
