Lloyd’s looks resilient under stress — but 1 January renewals may test pricing discipline.

Today’s digest brings together a strong capital and resilience signal with softer reinsurance conditions. For London Market teams, the practical watchpoints are how renewal pressure shapes underwriting decisions and whether property valuations reflect rebuilding costs.

1) Lloyd’s PRA stress test - Why it matters: Lloyd’s remained able to meet valid claims and continue operating in simulated cyber, market and catastrophe losses, while post-event recapitalisation remained a challenge.
2) Property-catastrophe renewals - Why it matters: Hannover Re expects at least the same level of rate softening as in 2026 at 1 January 2027, pointing to continued pricing pressure.
3) Reinsurance capacity and pricing - Why it matters: Record global reinsurance capital of $800bn and double-digit price reductions on mid-year property-cat placements could mean a heavier renewal workload for cedants.
4) Commercial property replacement costs - Why it matters: One example put projected rebuilding costs at $7m against a $3m insured value, underlining the potential consequences of weak valuation inputs.
