ILS is more than a side story for the London Market today: Catastrophe bonds and third-party capital are part of the strategic conversation.

For brokers and underwriters, the update also points to potential shifts in specialty distribution and transactional risk. Four stories worth a closer look:

1) Nepal’s proposed earthquake cat bond - Why it matters: The planned three-year parametric programme could add sovereign disaster risk financing, with a proposed US$80m–US$190m target.
2) Hannover Re’s view of third-party capital - Why it matters: The reinsurer sees investor capital as a strategic partner and opportunity, not just supplementary capacity.
3) Link2Market’s new wholesale broker - Why it matters: Its Lloyd’s broker and coverholder model could give brokers direct access to property and casualty binder capacity for non-standard risks.
4) Optio’s Pinnacle Specialty Insurance launch - Why it matters: The Amsterdam-based MGA plans to offer W&I and tax liability limits up to €20m, plus contingent risk cover up to €10m.
