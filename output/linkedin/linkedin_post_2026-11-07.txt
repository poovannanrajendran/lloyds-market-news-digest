London Market capacity is expanding as reinsurance pricing comes under pressure.

Today’s digest spans new Lloyd’s capital, catastrophe risk and changing specialty-market conditions. The implications for capacity and renewal strategy are worth watching.

1) Cortina, Aon and Blackstone’s Lloyd’s syndicate 1956 - Why it matters: With QBE as managing agent and a planned 1 January 2027 underwriting start subject to approval, it could add dedicated reinsurance capacity.
2) MS Amlin’s proposed first catastrophe bond - Why it matters: The syndicate is seeking $100 million, adding to Lloyd’s alternative risk-transfer activity if the bond proceeds.
3) Secondary-peril losses in North America - Why it matters: Severe convective storms, wildfire, winter storms and flood may prompt insurers to reassess programmes focused on rare, large-scale catastrophes.
4) US E&S growth slows - Why it matters: ALIRT reports 2.8% annualised direct premium growth in the first half of 2026, a shift that may affect specialty carriers, brokers and delegated underwriting businesses.
