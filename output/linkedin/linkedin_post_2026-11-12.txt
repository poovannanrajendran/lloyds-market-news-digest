London Market capacity is expanding—but the shape of that capacity is changing.

Today’s digest points to new routes into reinsurance, changing specialty structures and persistent catastrophe losses. For brokers and underwriters, the practical question is how these shifts could affect placements and risk appetite.

1) Cortina’s Lloyd’s syndicate launch - Why it matters: Expected to underwrite from 1 January 2027, it is designed to provide dedicated, multi-year reinsurance capacity backed by third-party capital.
2) Intact’s planned UK and European specialty consolidation - Why it matters: Subject to regulatory approval, the structure could let European risks be underwritten from London through a more streamlined broker route.
3) Ursa Re’s increased cat bond target - Why it matters: The California Earthquake Authority has raised its target from US$600 million to US$900 million for fully collateralised earthquake reinsurance, though final terms are not reported.
4) Insured catastrophe losses above $100bn for the tenth consecutive year - Why it matters: Gallagher Re’s reported figure keeps catastrophe exposure management in focus for underwriting and reinsurance planning.
