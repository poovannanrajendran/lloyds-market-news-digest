Data centres: the new concentration risk reshaping re/insurance

Source: www.globalreinsurance.com · Score: 0.85
Why it matters: Discusses insurance risk and BI exposure for data centres—core Lloyd's market topic.
  • AI-driven data-centre build-out will create unprecedented concentrations of high-value, insurable assets (global capacity expected to double by 2026–2030; up to ~$3tn investment and individual sites potentially worth tens of billions), producing large, correlated exposures across multiple lines.
  • Natural catastrophe and climate risk are major aggregation drivers: MS Amlin finds a large share of planned US projects sit in severe convective storm, hurricane, earthquake or winter-storm zones (e.g., ~51% in SCS states, hundreds of billions of dollars at risk); in Europe, pluvial flooding, heat stress and water scarcity plus design weaknesses (flat roofs, low-placed kit) raise operational and physical loss potential.
  • Physical attack and sabotage risks (rockets, missiles, uncrewed aerial systems/drones) alter probable-maximum-loss profiles: relatively small, targeted strikes on power, cooling or connectivity can trigger outsized business-interruption losses without total structural destruction, requiring engineering-led threat and loss modelling.
  • Exposure is lifecycle- and supply-chain-wide (financing, permitting, transport, construction, commissioning to operation) and cuts across property, BI, cyber, terrorism/political violence and specialty classes — requiring granular site-selection analysis, tighter design/mitigation standards, aggregation/PML modelling and revised underwriting/wording to manage correlated, systemic concentrations.
