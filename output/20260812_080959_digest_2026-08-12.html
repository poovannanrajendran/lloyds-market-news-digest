Source: www.insurancejournal.com · Score: 0.92
Why it matters: Discusses climate-induced risks (heat, drought, wildfires) and insurer losses in Europe, directly relevant to Lloyd's market exposure.

  • Short‑term insured losses were below trend in H1 2026 ($42bn) but Swiss Re warns the long‑term upward trajectory remains intact — insurers should urgently reassess underwriting, accumulation controls, pricing, CAT models and adaptation/resilience strategies for heat‑driven risks.

Legionnaires’ outbreaks spark risk management concerns - Business Insurance

Source: www.businessinsurance.com · Score: 0.78
Why it matters: Discusses risk management and insurance coverage implications of Legionnaires’ outbreaks, including policy exclusions.
  • New York law now mandates twice-yearly cooling tower certifications, faster reporting of serious health threats, stricter maintenance requirements and longer recordkeeping — violations tied to fatality/serious injury can carry civil penalties up to $12,500.
  • Recent outbreaks (Central Harlem July 2025: 7 deaths, 118 cases; Upper East Side: 7 deaths, 92 positives and dozens hospitalized) have spurred high-profile litigation alleging failures to inspect and maintain cooling towers.
  • Coverage complexity: Legionella contamination generally does not constitute 'property damage' for property policies; commercial general liability can be excluded or subject to low sublimits depending on communicable-disease/pollution language; site pollution liability (SPL) policies are often the most reliable source of coverage for bodily injury, defense, business interruption and remediation, though with higher retentions.
  • Practical actions for insurers/brokers: review clients’ inspection/maintenance and recordkeeping protocols, audit CGL exclusions/sublimits and SPL placements/retentions, consider endorsements or expanded pollution wording, and assess litigation/defense exposure tied to maintenance practices and policy wording.
