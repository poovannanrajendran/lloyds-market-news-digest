Marsh Re warns of growing gap between secondary peril losses and reinsurance protection - Reinsurance News

Source: www.reinsurancene.ws · Score: 0.78
Why it matters: Discusses reinsurance market pressures, capacity and modelling for catastrophe losses, relevant to Lloyd's market dynamics.
  • Marsh Re says secondary perils—including severe thunderstorms, wildfires, winter storms and flooding—are a major contributor to insured catastrophe claims across North America, while reinsurance programmes often remain focused on large-scale disasters.
  • US insured losses from severe convective storms have exceeded USD 30 billion annually since 2020, with four years above USD 40 billion. The January 2025 Los Angeles wildfires produced the highest insured wildfire losses recorded to date.
  • Marsh Re says post-2023 market adjustments have generally increased the catastrophe losses insurers must retain before reinsurance responds, exposing primary insurers to greater costs when events recur.
  • The report recommends validating external catastrophe models against insurers’ own claims data, collecting more detailed property and protection information, and reviewing reinsurance structures to address cumulative losses from repeated events. It notes that suitable cover at commercially acceptable prices can be difficult to obtain.

Agriculture in an El Niño year: the view from China and India

Source: www.globalreinsurance.com · Score: 0.78
Why it matters: Discusses reinsurance pricing, capacity, and public-private risk-sharing for agriculture in India/China—relevant to Lloyd's market.
  • China’s nationwide agricultural insurance scheme receives close to 80% of premiums in central-government subsidies; around 30 qualified insurers underwrite it, with reinsurance including policy-oriented cessions and capacity from more than 60 market reinsurers. India’s crop scheme covers production risk, with premiums shared by central government, states and farmers; insurers bid for district clusters and place reinsurance through open-market treaties.
  • China is shifting towards fuller protection for major grain crops, including full-cost and revenue cover. In India, some states use 80:110 and 60:130 cup-and-cap models that share premium surpluses and losses between insurers and government, changing insurers’ retained risk and reinsurance needs.
  • The article reports that India’s 2026 south-west monsoon ended with rainfall more than 12% below average; crops on around 5.5 million hectares in Karnataka were reported damaged, while around 9 million hectares were affected in Maharashtra. Huq says implied rates fell by around 30% while sums insured rose by around 28% during the 2026 Kharif season, making rate adequacy a central concern for capacity providers.
  • In China, natural disasters affected 1.70 million hectares of cropland in the first half of 2026, including 852,100 hectares affected by floods and geological disasters; direct economic losses totalled RMB 42.1 billion. Xin says risk analysis should account for location, event sequences and regional accumulation, and use climate projections separately from historical experience to stress-test portfolios.
