ILS and broader securitisation key to absorbing nat cat risk: Schroders Capital - Artemis.bm

Source: www.artemis.bm · Score: 0.85
Why it matters: Covers catastrophe risk, insurance-linked securities, risk transfer, and capital market enablement—topics central to Lloyd's market.
  • Rising insured losses, constrained reinsurance capacity and greater economic exposure (more assets/people in hazard zones, higher rebuild costs, climate-driven hazard changes) are shifting risk onto households, lenders, capital markets, institutional investors and governments — creating new transmission channels and demand for capital.
  • Insurance-linked securities (ILS) and catastrophe bonds are highlighted as efficient, collateralised mechanisms to absorb peak nat‑cat risk, improve price discovery and expand capacity; they offer attractive, low‑correlation risk premia but carry model, parameter and tail risks and can withdraw when risk/return is unfavorable.

  • Private equity and insurtech can complement ILS by mobilising capital to close the global protection gap (especially in emerging markets) through investment in risk origination, distribution, data platforms and adaptation technologies, addressing structural market failures in modelling, affordability and access.

Washington court reinstates verdict for Tacoma in firefighter comp dispute - Business Insurance

Source: www.businessinsurance.com · Score: 0.68
Why it matters: Insurance/claims relevance: court ruling on a workers' compensation dispute with potential impact on liability and discovery practices.
  • Washington Court of Appeals reinstated a jury verdict for the City of Tacoma in Varney v. City of Tacoma, concluding the city’s late production of more than 950 pages did not sufficiently prejudice the plaintiffs to justify a new trial.
  • The appeals court reversed the trial court’s imposition of liability as a discovery sanction, upholding the jury’s no‑liability finding while leaving open the possibility of monetary sanctions against the city.
  • Case background: firefighter Tony Varney suffered a hypertensive emergency and stroke at work (2009), was placed on a medical pension (2018), and he and his wife sued the city in 2019 alleging abuse of process and outrage related to the workers’ compensation handling.
  • Key takeaways for insurers and risk managers: courts require demonstrable prejudice before granting new trials for late discovery; imposing liability as a sanction is disfavored; document and preserve any claimed prejudice, prioritize timely and complete production, and consider monetary sanctions and litigation/reserve implications when discovery misconduct occurs.
