Insurers fight claims tied to ‘tax scam’ investments - Business Insurance

Source: www.businessinsurance.com · Score: 23.86
  • Scope and scale: Insurers (including Liberty Mutual) are defending about 20 lawsuits seeking coverage under tax‑liability policies tied to syndicated conservation easement transactions; plaintiffs estimate more than $1 billion of policy limits at issue after the IRS disallowed deductions that contributed to >$36 billion in disputed deductions and audits of ~28,000 investors (transaction effectively shut down in 2022).
  • Product and placement profile: The cases involve indemnity‑only tax liability insurance often purchased by investor partnerships (roughly one‑third did so); many policies were bound after 2016 when the IRS publicly identified the schemes. Policies were placed via MGAs formed to write these risks (e.g., Volante) with primary and excess layers from carriers such as Liberty, Fidelis, AmTrust and Allianz.
  • Alleged underwriting exposure and defense posture: Plaintiffs contend underwriters reviewed PPMs, appraisals and tax opinions and therefore understood the risk; carriers are disputing coverage and litigating based on policy wording, timing, and underwriting diligence. Example: Ashwood Gap (Alabama) alleges a $10.3M purchase produced a $52.3M deduction based on aggressive appraisals, with a $10.3M policy limit at issue.
  • Practical implications for insurers: anticipate protracted coverage litigation and potential aggregation/concentration risk and reputational/regulatory scrutiny; recommended actions include reviewing tax‑liability policy wordings and exclusions, preserving and documenting file‑level due diligence, reassessing MGA/placement controls, stress‑testing limit accumulation and reinsurance arrangements for similar systemic tax‑scheme exposures.

Delaware high court rejects worker’s dental comp claim - Business Insurance

Source: www.businessinsurance.com · Score: 11.93
  • Delaware Supreme Court upheld denial of workers’ compensation for lower-teeth damage claimed in 2023 after a 2012 workplace head injury, finding the claimant failed to prove causation.
  • An earlier Industrial Accident Board finding that upper-teeth fractures were work-related (and paid by the insurer) did not preclude a different conclusion on the separate lower-teeth claim; the court noted distinct causation issues.
  • The IAB credited the employer’s expert over the claimant’s dentist — who tied wear to crowns and lack of care while in a coma — and the courts found substantial evidence supported the denial.
  • Implications for insurers: late-onset or separate dental claims arising after long time gaps can be defensible when intervening factors and competing expert opinions undermine causal linkage to the workplace injury.

Thousands of European Wind, Solar Power Systems Exposed Online: Dutch Researchers

Source: www.insurancejournal.com · Score: 0.80
Why it matters: Cyber risk exposure of European energy infrastructure with potential insurance implications.
  • Researchers identified 8,547 internet-facing systems linked to 7,942 solar and 605 wind sites across 35 European countries; many are admin/login pages and some operational interfaces (including Start/Stop/Reset controls). Researchers estimate around 181 sites could be fully controllable if exploited.
  • Direct cyber‑physical risk: exposed interfaces could enable attackers to stop turbines, induce outages, stress equipment or cause physical damage — a December 2025 attack on 30 Polish sites is cited as a real-world precedent for sabotage and business interruption.
  • Concentration risk: exposures are regionally clustered (notably Spain: 2,766 solar; Greece: 1,860 solar; Germany: 672 solar and 212 wind; Italy: 192 wind), creating aggregation risk for insurers and reinsurers writing renewable-energy portfolios.
  • Recommended insurer actions: tighten cyber-physical underwriting and evidence requirements (network segmentation, removal of admin interfaces from the internet, MFA, patching, asset inventory), update BI and physical-damage loss models, mandate incident-response plans and third‑party risk controls, and consider pricing, exclusions or reinsurance for systemic exposure.
