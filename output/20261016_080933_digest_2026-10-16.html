Hurricane Isaias unlikely to affect Jan 1 reinsurance renewals, says Gallagher Re - Reinsurance News

Source: www.reinsurancene.ws · Score: 0.80
Why it matters: Discusses Isaias' expected impact on reinsurance renewals, capacity and pricing—directly relevant to Lloyd's market dynamics.
  • Gallagher Re says Hurricane Isaias is unlikely to affect 1 January reinsurance renewal discussions. It expects insured losses, including the anticipated NFIP loss, to be at the lower end of the low-to-mid-single-digit billions of US dollars.
  • The firm cites the storm’s track through a less populated part of Florida’s western Panhandle, limited exposure to its strongest winds and a well-capitalised market. It expects even a multi-billion-dollar loss not to materially change pricing expectations, capacity availability or market sentiment heading into 2027.
  • Isaias made landfall near Destin on 9 October as a weakening Category 2 hurricane, after peaking as a Category 3. Gallagher Re reports eight storm-related deaths, nearly 900,000 customers without power at the peak, and at least 21 confirmed tornadoes across the Southeast.
  • Gallagher Re says Isaias occurred during an exceptionally quiet Atlantic season: by mid-October there had been nine named storms, one hurricane and one major hurricane, with ACE of 18.0, compared with a 2016–2025 average of 130.6.

Source: www.insurancejournal.com · Score: 0.78
Why it matters: Covers cyber insurance coverage, premium implications, and new products for physical losses—relevant to Lloyd's market.

UK liability claims growth could rise further as legal reforms are reviewed, says Swiss Re - Reinsurance News

Source: www.reinsurancene.ws · Score: 0.75
Why it matters: Covers UK liability claims drivers and proposed reforms that affect insurers, with potential implications for the Lloyd's market.
  • Swiss Re says non-economic factors added around five to six percentage points to UK liability claims growth in 2024 and accounted for about half of growth since 2020, the fifth consecutive year of elevated liability excess inflation.
  • The report links claims pressure partly to legislative and procedural changes, litigation funding, claimant recruitment practices, collective redress and the Building Safety Act 2022. It says litigation funding can make more cases viable but may reduce incentives for early settlement in some circumstances.
  • Potential reforms include reversing the effect of the 2023 PACCAR ruling on certain funding agreements, reviewing collective action procedures and modernising product liability law for digital technologies and AI. At the time of the report, legislation on PACCAR remained pending, and Swiss Re says the impact will depend on the reforms’ final design and implementation.
  • Swiss Re identifies public, product, professional and cyber liability as areas that could be particularly affected, with construction, engineering, AI and technology among potentially significant sectors. It says environmental liability could also generate large claims, while employers’ liability and medical malpractice may be less exposed.

Source: www.globalreinsurance.com · Score: 0.72
Why it matters: Contains insured loss estimates and reinsurance market implications relevant to Lloyd's.
  • Verisk estimates onshore insured property losses of $1bn–$2bn, including wind and privately insured storm surge. Gallagher Re’s broader estimate, which also includes inland flooding, tornadoes and National Flood Insurance Program losses, is at the lower end of the low-to-mid-single-digit billions.
  • Verisk expects wind to account for most insured losses. The most severe wind damage was reported west of landfall, particularly in Santa Rosa and north-eastern Escambia counties, including roof and structural damage, fallen trees and debris.

  • Gallagher Re expects little impact on 1 January reinsurance renewals, citing record levels of traditional and alternative capacity and saying the event will not materially alter pricing expectations or capacity availability.
