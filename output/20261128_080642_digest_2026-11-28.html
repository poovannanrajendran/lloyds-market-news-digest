Lloyd’s targets more joined-up oversight as 2027 business plan points to disciplined growth - Reinsurance News

Source: www.reinsurancene.ws · Score: 0.90
Why it matters: Details Lloyd's 2027 market oversight framework and business plan.
  • Lloyd’s plans to bring supervision of underwriting risk, solvency, and conduct and governance into a more coordinated approach in 2027, aiming for oversight that is consistent, proportionate and predictable while maintaining robust standards.
  • The 2027 business plan targets £74.1 billion in gross written premium, 10% headline growth against the Q2 forecast, and a 92.4% net combined ratio; like-for-like growth is expected to fall by 1.5%.
  • Against the Q2 forecast baseline, the plan attributes growth to existing syndicates’ new business (2.1%), platform transfers (0.8%), facilities and portfolio business (2.1%), reinsurance captives (2.9%) and new entrants (3.2%), taking the plan to 109.6% of the baseline.
  • Lloyd’s says profitable growth and underwriting discipline take priority over volume, and expects managing agents to proactively manage the underwriting cycle and wider business risks; it says it will intervene where it believes this is not happening.

Munich Re and Aon use new methodology to identify more than 100 cyber accumulation scenarios - Reinsurance News

Source: www.reinsurancene.ws · Score: 0.78
Why it matters: Describes a joint framework for identifying cyber accumulation risk by Munich Re and Aon; relevant to Lloyd's market risk and underwriting practices.
  • Munich Re and Aon used a structured methodology to identify more than 100 high-level cyber accumulation scenarios, including risks less frequently considered by the insurance industry.
  • The framework uses a scenario tree built around five dimensions: failure context, affected asset, threat, compromised security objective and insurance consequences. It distinguishes single points of failure from distributed points of failure and comprises 72 possible pathways.
  • Scenarios include established risks such as self-replicating malware, software supply chain incidents and major IT service provider outages, as well as time server and certificate authority failures, hardware-based attacks, severe non-malicious incidents and attacks on critical industrial supply chains.
  • The companies ranked scenarios by potential footprint, number of companies affected and severity, while noting that these subjective assessments indicate importance rather than provide precise measures.

Source: www.artemis.bm · Score: 0.78
Why it matters: Final PERILS industry loss estimate for Australian convective storms; includes insurer data and property/motor breakdown.
  • PERILS’ final industry loss estimate for severe convective storms affecting Queensland and New South Wales from 21 to 27 November 2025 is AU$3.132 billion, around 6% higher than its third estimate.
  • The estimate is based on data from insurers representing the majority of the Australian market and covers property and motor losses. Property accounted for 89% of the total and motor for 11%.
  • Queensland accounted for 93% of the industry loss; New South Wales accounted for 7%.
  • The most significant losses were linked to storms on 24 November, when hailstones up to 14cm in diameter struck parts of South East Queensland. The event also brought damaging winds and heavy rainfall.
