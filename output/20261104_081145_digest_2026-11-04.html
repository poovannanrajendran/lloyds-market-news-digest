EFAMA says cat bonds a "precious diversifier", UCITS eligibility should remain unchanged - Artemis.bm

Source: www.artemis.bm · Score: 0.82
Why it matters: EU policy debate on UCITS EAD and catastrophe bonds (ILS) affecting market access and diversification.
  • EFAMA has urged EU policymakers to leave catastrophe bonds’ eligibility under the UCITS Eligible Assets Directive unchanged, arguing that they provide diversification and support natural-disaster risk transfer. ESMA recommended in June 2025 that catastrophe bonds should not be eligible assets in UCITS funds; under its proposed approach, they could be held only within a 10% alternative-assets limit.
  • EFAMA cites ESMA analysis showing catastrophe bonds’ correlations of 0.19–0.29 with major asset classes. It also says their estimated annual secondary-market turnover is 23%, compared with 33% for investment-grade and 19% for high-yield European corporate bonds.
  • UCITS catastrophe bond funds had nearly $21.8bn in combined assets under management at the end of September 2026. EFAMA says direct retail investors account for roughly 1% of their investor base, with access largely through institutional, professional and intermediated channels.
  • EFAMA warns that material changes to the rules could require the assets in catastrophe-bond-focused UCITS funds to be reallocated, converted or relaunched in another structure, or potentially lead to fund closures. New FCA rules bring ESG rating providers into UK regulation for the first time

Source: www.fca.org.uk · Score: 0.72
Why it matters: Regulatory changes to ESG rating providers could influence insurers' risk assessment and investment decisions in the Lloyd's market.
  • The FCA’s new framework brings ESG rating providers into regulation for the first time, with requirements covering transparency, governance, systems and controls, conflicts of interest, and engagement with users and rated entities.
  • Providers operating in the UK market must apply for FCA authorisation from 24 June 2027. The framework takes effect on 29 June 2028; from then, firms need the relevant permission to provide ESG ratings unless exempt.
  • The rules are intended to improve the transparency, reliability and comparability of ESG ratings. They are designed to be proportionate to firms’ size and activities and do not prescribe a particular sustainability view or methodology.
  • The FCA has published guidance and says it will offer webinars and its Pre-Application Support Service. Firms that may be in scope are advised to review the rules, assess whether they need authorisation and begin preparing.
