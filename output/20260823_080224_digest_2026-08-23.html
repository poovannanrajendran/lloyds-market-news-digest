IRB (Re) renames special purpose insurer and LRS issuer Andrina, to become IRB (SSPE) - Artemis.bm

Source: www.artemis.bm · Score: 0.85
Why it matters: Brazil ILS regime activity, SSPE issuances, and IRB (Re) rebranding to IRB (SSPE) impacting the market.
  • IRB (Re) has renamed its special-purpose insurer Andrina SSPE to IRB (SSPE) to align the vehicle with the group brand — stressing continuity of function and signaling commitment to using capital markets to support its reinsurance business.
  • IRB (Re) was the first sponsor to use Brazil’s SSPE regime and executed the country’s inaugural LRS/ILS issuance in May 2025, effectively launching the domestic insurance-linked securities market.
  • Market activity has since accelerated: Galapagos Capital completed a R$100m LRS in Dec 2025 and subsequent follow-on issuances (including a R$126m broadly distributed deal in May 2026), and new issuers such as Ariel SSPE have entered the market.
  • Implication for insurers/reinsurers: Brazil’s LRS/ILS framework is gaining traction as a viable capital-markets route to collateralize and transfer insurance and reinsurance risk, and is expected to expand as investor appetite for risk-linked returns grows.

Returns drive investor appetite, third-party reinsurance capital increasingly important: AM Best - Artemis.bm

Source: www.artemis.bm · Score: 0.80
Why it matters: Discusses ILS and third-party reinsurance capital influencing global reinsurance capacity, relevant to Lloyd's market.
  • Strong ILS performance is driving investor allocations and third‑party reinsurance capacity—AM Best (with Guy Carpenter) raised its end‑2025 estimate to $123bn (from $120bn) and forecasts ~$130bn by end‑2026.
  • Catastrophe bond issuance remains robust: record 144A issuance of $25.6bn in 2025, nearly $18bn in H1 2026, and total outstanding ILS market ~ $65.6bn at end‑June 2026.
  • ILS attraction stems from attractive risk‑adjusted returns, portfolio diversification and recycling of investor capital, making third‑party capital an increasingly important component of global reinsurance capacity.
  • Broader reinsurance market fundamentals remain resilient—traditional reinsurers produced strong underwriting and investment results, retained capital while returning capital to shareholders, and are forming more mature partnerships with alternative capital providers that help exceed capital costs.

RenRe third-party capital ILS assets managed hit $8.54bn at H1 2026, up 5.5% in last year - Artemis.bm

Source: www.artemis.bm · Score: 0.80
Why it matters: RenaissanceRe's ILS and third-party capital activity and market dynamics relevant to Lloyd's.
  • RenaissanceRe Capital Partners’ third‑party AUM rose ~5.5% year‑on‑year to $8.54bn at June 30, 2026, but remains below the end‑2025 record peak (~$9.08bn) after a period of meaningful capital returns to investors.
  • Growth has slowed as RenRe deliberately returned capital and resized vehicles to align third‑party capacity with the softened property catastrophe market, prioritizing underwriting capacity management over scaling AUM.
  • Balance‑sheet/joint‑venture vehicles drove most activity: DaVinci Re third‑party AUM was $3.69bn (up from $3.35bn on Jan 1), Vermeer Re $1.89bn (from $1.77bn), and underwriting returns contributed to mid‑year growth.
  • ILS and cat‑bond strategies were flatter: Medici fund stable at $1.44bn, total catastrophe bond assets (including RenRe stakes) $2.51bn with $2.01bn of third‑party cat‑bond AUM slightly down quarter‑on‑quarter; Fontana (casualty/specialty JV) rose to about $930m (+~$160m y/y).
