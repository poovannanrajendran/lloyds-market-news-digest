Binance's $6.3 Billion Hack: How Crypto Insurance Works

Source: www.insurancejournal.com · Score: 0.84
Why it matters: Discusses crypto insurance and underwriting in the Lloyd's/Bermuda market context.
  • Digital asset insurance is a niche market, offered largely by specialist insurers in the Lloyd’s market and Bermuda. Cover can address theft, cyberattacks, technology failures and other business risks, with policy terms varying by asset storage and how a breach occurs.
  • Crypto losses may be met through insurance, company capital or emergency reserves. Cover depends on policy limits, deductibles and exclusions; losses above insured amounts must be met through other resources or recovery arrangements.
  • Common exclusions include value fluctuations, policyholder fraud and unsecured or negligent key management. Standard crime policies generally do not cover cryptocurrency losses, while cyber policies may exclude the value of stolen or transferred assets unless specific digital asset cover is added.
  • Insurers assess how a breach occurred, which systems were compromised and whether security procedures were followed. Whether assets were stolen by an external hacker or deliberately transferred by an employee can affect which policy responds.

Global insured natural catastrophe losses reach $91bn in 9M’26, lowest since 2019: Aon - Artemis.bm

Source: www.artemis.bm · Score: 0.80
Why it matters: Catastrophe loss data and risk trends relevant to the Lloyd's insurance/underwriting market.
  • Aon estimated global insured natural-catastrophe losses at $91bn in the first nine months of 2026, below the five-year average of $135bn but slightly above the 21st-century average of $88bn. Third-quarter insured losses were $29bn, 31% below the 21st-century average.
  • Economic losses reached $204bn, the lowest first-nine-month total since 2015 and 31% below the 21st-century average. There were 42 events with economic losses above $1bn and 23 with insured losses above $1bn, both exceeding their respective long-term averages.
  • The protection gap was 55%. Severe convective storms were the costliest peril for economic and insured losses, and 62% of global insured losses were recorded in the US; US insured losses totalled $56bn, including more than $43bn from severe convective storms.
  • A European drought from May to September generated more than $8.5bn in insured losses, making it the costliest insured event of the year to date. The Venezuela earthquake was the largest economic-loss event, estimated at $25bn.
