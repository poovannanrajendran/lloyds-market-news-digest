Insurers must cover Silicon Valley Bank’s loss in loan fraud - Business Insurance

Source: www.businessinsurance.com · Score: 0.78
Why it matters: Involves insurance coverage disputes and policy interpretations by major insurers (Chubb, W.R. Berkley) over SVB loan fraud losses, pertinent to Lloyd's market.
  • Federal judge granted summary judgment to the FDIC (receiver for SVB) requiring units of Chubb and W.R. Berkley to cover losses from a $150M credit line obtained with forged partnership/subscription agreements; SVB funded about $95M in 2021 and claims losses of more than $70M.
  • Insurers’ denial based on absence of “original” physical documents (SVB received PDFs by email) was rejected: the bond’s ambiguity is construed for the insured and the court held that electronic/electronically‑transmitted documents can constitute “originals” when they serve as source documents for loan transactions.
  • Court found the forged agreements qualified as covered “guarantees” and “security agreements,” that forgery was the sole cause of the loss, and the bank acted in good faith—therefore coverage was triggered despite the fraud.
  • Key implications for insurers: ambiguous policy language will be interpreted for policyholders—explicitly define “original” and address electronic documents; reassess wording on guarantees/security agreements and forgery exclusions; review limits and layer exposure (federal bond $15M + Berkley $15M vs. >$70M loss); and strengthen underwriting, endorsements and fraud controls for transactions relying on electronic documentation.
